About the data: USDA FoodData Central

Every number on this site comes from one published source. This page names it and says what it covers.

The source

The numbers come from USDA FoodData Central, the food composition database of the United States Department of Agriculture's Agricultural Research Service. Within it, we used the SR Legacy data type: the National Nutrient Database for Standard Reference, Legacy Release of April 2018. The USDA describes SR Legacy as the primary food composition data type in the United States for decades and states that this is its final release, which will not be updated. We downloaded the CSV files in November 2026.

How the values were obtained

The USDA says its values are derived from chemical analyses, calculation and imputation, and the published literature. Each value in the download has a data-point count and, for many, a minimum and maximum, which we do not display. We use the mean values unchanged and round them for display: whole numbers for energy, minerals and most vitamins, one decimal for grams and for iron and zinc.

What is included here

We chose about 700 foods that are commonly cooked at home: vegetables, fruits, pulses and dals, nuts and seeds, grains and flours, dairy products and eggs, spices, oils, sugars, fish and some poultry, in their raw or plain boiled or roasted forms. We left out branded and restaurant foods, baby foods and most prepared dishes, because their values belong to a particular recipe. Many foods eaten in India (roti, paneer, most regional vegetables and dishes) have no USDA record and are not here. Foods with Indian common names show the name as an alias.

Licence and credit

The USDA states that FoodData Central data are in the public domain, not copyrighted, and published under CC0 1.0 Universal, and asks users to credit FoodData Central as the source (statement read at fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide on 13 November 2026). The suggested citation is: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service. FoodData Central, 2019. fdc.nal.usda.gov. The text, layout and calculations on this site are ours; this site is independent and not connected with the USDA.

What this site adds

Scaling to a portion or to the amount you type, conversion of kilocalories to kilojoules (x 4.184), the approximate energy split by the 4-4-9 factors, rankings within a group of foods and plain-language explanations. It adds no health claims, no daily-value percentages and no advice.

Is the data up to date?

SR Legacy is frozen at April 2018. Basic foods change slowly, but varieties and processing do, so treat values as typical.

Where can I see the original?

In FoodData Central at fdc.nal.usda.gov: search for the USDA name shown at the top of each food page.
