Nutrition per 100 g or per serving?

The same food looks very different per 100 g and per serving. Both are correct; they answer different questions.

Two ways to state the same thing

A nutrition table can say how much of each nutrient there is in a fixed weight (usually 100 grams) or in a portion the maker or the database chooses (a cup, a medium fruit, a slice, a tablespoon). Neither is wrong. They answer two different questions.

  • Per 100 g is a fair way to compare foods, because the weight is the same for all of them. It is the standard in most countries and on this site's main tables.
  • Per portion tells you what you take in when you eat a typical amount. It is more useful for planning a meal, but it depends on how big the portion is, and that is a choice.

Why they can seem to disagree

Take an example from the USDA data: raw almonds have 579 kcal per 100 g, while raw spinach has 23 kcal per 100 g. Per 100 g, almonds have about 25 times as much energy as spinach. But nobody eats 100 g of almonds as a snack, and a cup of raw spinach weighs only about 30 g, so a cup of spinach has about 7 kcal. A handful of almonds (about 28 g) has about 160 kcal. Per portion the gap between them is smaller than per 100 g, but still large. Comparing per 100 g tells you about the food; comparing per portion tells you about the eating habit.

Converting between them

To go from per 100 g to a portion, multiply by the portion weight and divide by 100.

StepWorkingResult
FoodRaw almonds579 kcal per 100 g
Portion28 g
Energy579 x 28 / 100about 162 kcal

To go back, divide by the portion weight and multiply by 100. The calculator on the home page does this for any weight in grams, and the food pages list the household portions the USDA recorded, with energy, protein, fat and carbohydrate worked out for each.

Watch for these traps

  • Portion size on packets is chosen by the maker and may be smaller than what people eat. Check the weight, not just the word "serving".
  • Raw and cooked foods have very different values per 100 g because cooking adds or removes water. See raw and cooked values.
  • Dry and prepared products: a table for the dry powder is not for the drink or porridge made from it.
  • Rounding. Values per portion are scaled from per 100 g, so small rounding differences appear.

This site gives information only; it does not tell you how much of anything to eat.

Is per 100 g the same as per 100 ml?

Only for water-like liquids. For milk or oil the weight of 100 ml differs from 100 g.

Why do some tables use per 100 g and others per serving?

Per 100 g is the legal comparison unit in many countries; per serving is common in the US, where the serving is also defined by the maker.
