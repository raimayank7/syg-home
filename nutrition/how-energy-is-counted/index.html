How the calories in food are counted

Food energy is not measured by burning every food. It is mostly calculated from protein, fat and carbohydrate.

Calories and kilojoules

Food energy is stated in kilocalories (kcal, often just called "calories") or kilojoules (kJ). The two are units of the same thing: one kilocalorie is 4.184 kilojoules. A food with 100 kcal has 418 kJ. This site shows kilocalories in tables and kilojoules next to the energy figure on each food page, worked out by multiplying by 4.184.

The 4-4-9 rule

US food labelling rules (21 CFR 101.9(c)(1)(i), read on eCFR on 13 November 2026) allow the energy of a food to be worked out from its nutrients using general factors of 4, 4 and 9 calories per gram for protein, total carbohydrate and total fat, as described in USDA Handbook No. 74, or with more specific Atwater factors for individual foods. The same section also describes a variant that leaves out the non-digestible carbohydrates and sugar alcohols.

NutrientEnergy per gram (general factor)
Protein4 kcal
Carbohydrate4 kcal
Fat9 kcal

So the energy of a food can be estimated from the grams of each. For example, 100 g of a food with 20 g of protein, 50 g of carbohydrate and 10 g of fat gives roughly 20 x 4 + 50 x 4 + 10 x 9 = 80 + 200 + 90 = 370 kcal. The "energy split" on each food page of this site uses the general factors to show approximately how the energy divides between the three nutrients.

Why the totals do not always match

The regulation names more specific Atwater factors for particular foods, and a variant that leaves out non-digestible carbohydrate and sugar alcohols. As a result, if you apply 4-4-9 to a food page here, the answer can differ by a few per cent from the USDA energy figure shown. When the two disagree, the USDA figure is the published one. The 4-4-9 numbers are an approximation that is easy to check by hand.

Fibre and water

The USDA data list water as a component with no energy of its own, so a watery food is lower in energy per 100 g. The regulation's variant that excludes non-digestible carbohydrates is one reason the carbohydrate figure and the energy figure on a food page do not move together exactly.

What this page does not say

It does not say how much energy you need or how much to eat; that depends on age, size and activity, and it is a question for a doctor or dietitian. It explains only where the numbers come from.

Are "calories" and "kcal" the same?

Yes. The food calorie is the kilocalorie.

Why is fat 9 and not 4?

The general factor for fat (9) is more than twice that for protein or carbohydrate (4).
