Grains, flours and cereals: nutrition facts

38 foods, per 100 g.

This page lists the 38 foods of the group "Grains, flours and cereals" with their energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate and fibre per 100 grams, all from the USDA SR Legacy database. The median food here has 361 kcal per 100 g, with a range from 123 to 395 kcal, so a gram of one food can carry many times the energy of a gram of another. Open any food for its full table of minerals and vitamins and its household portions.

Comparing per 100 g is fair but not always practical: a serving of a leafy vegetable weighs far less than a serving of nuts. Use the portion tables on the food pages to compare what you actually handle in the kitchen. This is general information and not dietary advice.

Highest in this group (per 100 g)

  • Energy: Bread, crumbs, dry, grated, plain (395 kcal), Oats (389 kcal), Besan (gram flour, chickpea flour) (387 kcal), Millet flour (382 kcal), Cornflour (corn starch) (381 kcal)
  • Protein: Vital wheat gluten (75.2 g), Wheat germ, crude (23.1 g), Besan (gram flour, chickpea flour) (22.4 g), Oat bran, raw (17.3 g), Oats (16.9 g)
  • Fibre: Barley, pearled, raw (15.6 g), Oat bran, raw (15.4 g), Rye grain (15.1 g), Wheat germ, crude (13.2 g), Bulgur (dalia), dry (12.5 g)

From 100 grams to a real portion

The figures in the table are for 100 grams, which is a handy way to compare foods but not a normal portion. A 30 g portion of Bread, crumbs, dry, grated, plain, the highest in the group at 395 kcal per 100 g, supplies about 118 kcal, while 30 g of Rice, brown, long-grain, cooked, the lowest at 123 kcal per 100 g, supplies about 37 kcal. A 200 g portion would be 790 kcal and 246 kcal respectively. Scale the figures for the amount you eat, not the amount in the table.

For Bread, crumbs, dry, grated, plain, the energy comes roughly 74% from carbohydrate, 12% from fat and 14% from protein, using the general factors of 4, 9 and 4 kilocalories per gram. This is an approximation, since alcohol, fibre and other components also count, but it shows what kind of food it is: a high-carbohydrate, high-fat or high-protein one.
