The four food safety guidelines from FSIS

Storage times are only one part of food safety. FSIS lists four guidelines that apply at every step.

The guidelines

The FSIS page "Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart" (last updated 14 April 2025, read on 13 November 2026) says that safe steps in food handling, cooking and storage are essential in preventing foodborne illness, because you cannot see, smell or taste harmful bacteria that may cause illness. It lists four guidelines to follow in every step of food preparation:

  1. Clean: wash hands and surfaces often.
  2. Separate: separate raw meat from other foods.
  3. Cook: cook to the right temperature.
  4. Chill: refrigerate food promptly.

Cook to the right temperature

The same page lists minimum internal temperatures measured with a food thermometer: for example 145 degrees F (62.8 degrees C) for steaks, chops, roasts and fish, 160 degrees F (71.1 degrees C) for ground meats and eggs, and 165 degrees F (73.9 degrees C) for poultry, leftovers and casseroles. Several of the food pages on this site that have cooking rows in the FoodKeeper data show the safe minimum temperature that goes with them.

Chill promptly

The FSIS page's featured fact sheet on leftovers says to handle leftovers with care: make sure the food is cooked to a safe temperature and refrigerate any leftovers promptly. This site did not read a specific number of hours for chilling, so it gives none.

What this site does not do

It does not give advice for your particular case, and it does not say a food is safe because a time in the data has not passed. Use the FSIS and your local authority's guidance.

Source: USDA FSIS, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart, fsis.usda.gov, last updated 14 April 2025, read 13 November 2026. FoodKeeper data: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, FoodKeeper data (English JSON, data version 128, modified 6 September 2018), https://www.fsis.usda.gov/shared/data/EN/foodkeeper.json, listed on data.gov ("FSIS - FoodKeeper Data", dataset last updated 22 January 2025), read on 13 November 2026.

Does this apply outside the US?

These are FSIS guidelines; check the rules of your own country for local requirements.

Is a food thermometer needed?

FSIS says the minimum temperatures are measured with a food thermometer.
