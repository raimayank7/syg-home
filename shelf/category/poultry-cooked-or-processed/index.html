Poultry: Cooked or Processed: how long food keeps

6 foods from the FoodKeeper data.

Of the 6 foods, 5 have a refrigerator time in the data, 5 have a freezer time and 0 have a pantry time; the rest have only tips, or times counted another way. A food that has no time for a place is one where FoodKeeper gives none, which is different from saying that place is safe.

This category of the USDA FSIS FoodKeeper data contains 6 foods. For each one the table shows the first storage time the data list (refrigerator first, then pantry, then freezer), and the food's page gives every time, the counting point (purchase, opening or thawing) and the tips FSIS gives. Times are ranges and general guidance, not a promise about a particular package: read how to read the times first. Foods that are not in the FoodKeeper data are not listed, and this site has no number for them.

Shortest and longest times in this category

Judged by the first time listed for each food, the foods that keep for the shortest time are Cooked poultry dishes (3 to 4 days); Fried chicken (3 to 4 days); Rotisserie chicken (3 to 4 days); the ones that keep longest are Chicken nuggets, patties (1 to 3 months); Turkey bacon (2 weeks); Pate, poultry (7 days). The difference matters when you plan a shop or clear a fridge: the first group should be used or frozen soon, and the second can wait. Of the 6 foods, 6 have a time that can be compared in this way, and 0 are given as tips only or in a form that cannot be sorted by length.

Using this category

Storage times in the FoodKeeper data are guidance for quality and safety under normal conditions. A time counted from the date of purchase starts at the shop, not at the date on the package; a time counted from opening starts when the seal is broken; and a time counted from thawing starts when the food was taken out of the freezer. A refrigerator should be at or below 40 degrees Fahrenheit (4 degrees Celsius) and a freezer at 0 degrees Fahrenheit (minus 18 degrees Celsius), the temperatures FSIS names for food safety. If a food looks, smells or feels wrong, do not eat it whatever the table says. Open a food from the table to see all its times and tips.
