Three flower seasons

  • Winter annuals are sown in September and October in the plains and flower from December to March. This is the richest season: petunia, pansy, calendula, phlox and many more.
  • Summer annuals are sown in February and March and flower through the heat: zinnia, sunflower, cosmos, portulaca.
  • Monsoon annuals are sown in June and July and flower in the rains and after: balsam, cockscomb, marigold.

In the south, where winter is mild, the winter flowers are sown a month later. In the hills the order is reversed: winter flowers of the plains are sown in spring and bloom in summer.

Shrubs and climbers

Rose, hibiscus, jasmine (mogra), bougainvillea and ixora are bought as plants, not sown. The best time to plant them is the monsoon, with early spring as the second choice. Water them well for the first few weeks and they need little care afterwards.

Getting more flowers

  • Pinch out the growing tip of young annuals once. They branch and carry more blooms.
  • Remove faded flowers before they set seed.
  • Give at least five to six hours of sun. Most flowering plants make leaves, not flowers, in shade.
  • Feed lightly every two weeks once buds appear.

Questions people ask

Which flowers can I sow in October?

In the plains, October is the main month for winter flowers: calendula, petunia, pansy, dianthus, phlox, snapdragon, sweet pea, nasturtium, dahlia and marigold.

When should I sow marigold for Diwali?

In June or July. Marigold flowers about two to two and a half months after sowing, and keeps flowering through the festival season.

Which flowers grow in the Indian summer?

Zinnia, sunflower, cosmos, portulaca, cockscomb and globe amaranth all stand heat. Sow them in February and March.

When are roses pruned?

In the north Indian plains, roses are pruned around the middle of October, after the monsoon, and flower from December. In the hills they are pruned at the end of winter.
