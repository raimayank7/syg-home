About the data: the 2023 USDA hardiness zone map

Every zone on this site comes from one published table. This page names it and says how it is used.

The source

The zones come from the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (2023 edition), developed by the USDA Agricultural Research Service together with the PRISM Group at Oregon State University (OSU). The map is based on the 30-year period 1991 to 2020. The ZIP code table (one zone for each ZIP code area, 40,502 ZIP codes in the 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico) was downloaded from the PRISM Group's website (prism.oregonstate.edu) in November 2026.

Terms of use

According to the USDA, under a cooperative agreement OSU retains ownership of the datasets behind the 2012 and 2023 maps, and they may be freely reproduced and redistributed, with these conditions: any maps derived from the data must prominently display the USDA-ARS and OSU logos, and the data may not be altered unless there is an explicit disclaimer that it is not the official map. PRISM's terms ask that anyone using its data state its name, URL and the date of access. This site draws no map and does not alter the zones: it shows the ZIP code table unchanged. Credit: PRISM Group, Oregon State University, https://prism.oregonstate.edu, accessed 13 November 2026; USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, 2023, planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. This site is independent and is not the official map or connected with the USDA or Oregon State University. For the official map use planthardiness.ars.usda.gov.

What this site adds

A lookup by ZIP code, conversion of the Fahrenheit ranges to Celsius, counts of how many ZIP codes are in each zone and region (computed from the same table) and plain explanations paraphrasing the USDA's "How to Use the Maps" page (read 13 November 2026). It adds no plant lists and no frost dates, because those were not read from an official source.

Limits

  • One zone per ZIP code. Large ZIP code areas can include more than one zone on the official map.
  • Zones describe the average coldest night, not the extreme.
  • The table covers the US and Puerto Rico only.
  • The data reflect weather from 1991 to 2020 and will be updated by the USDA from time to time.

Source: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, "How to Use the Maps" (planthardiness.ars.usda.gov), read on 13 November 2026.

How often is the map updated?

The USDA published editions in 2012 and 2023. This site uses 2023.

Can I copy the table?

The USDA and PRISM allow free redistribution under the conditions above. Read them before reusing.
