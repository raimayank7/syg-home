Hardiness zone 5: temperatures, ZIP codes and meaning

Zone 5 covers average annual extreme winter minimums of -20 to -10 °F (-28.9 to -23.3 °C).

Plant hardiness zone 5 is one of the 13 zones of the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (2023 edition). It covers places where the coldest night of a typical winter, averaged over the years 1991 to 2020, falls between -20 and -10 degrees Fahrenheit (-28.9 to -23.3 degrees Celsius). Zone 5 is divided into two half zones: 5a, the colder half, and 5b, the warmer half, each spanning 5 degrees. The neighbouring zones are zone 4 (colder) and zone 6 (warmer).

The USDA ZIP code table lists 5,306 ZIP codes in zone 5, which makes it the 4th most common of the 13 zones by number of ZIP codes (the table has 40,502 in all). By region, the table has 5,258 ZIP codes in the 50 states and the District of Columbia (the "us" table), 48 ZIP codes in Alaska.

What it means for a gardener

A plant described as hardy to zone 5 is expected to survive the usual winter lows of this zone and warmer ones. If a plant tag lists a range of zones that includes zone 5, the plant is inside its range here. The zone is an average, not the coldest night ever: the USDA warns that plants at the cold limit of their range can be lost in a rare cold snap lasting only a day or two, and that wind, soil, moisture, snow, winter sunshine and planting position also decide survival. Zone numbers are used for perennial plants; annual plants only need a safe planting date, which is a matter of frost dates, not zones (see zones are not frost dates).

Questions

What temperature is zone 5?

The average annual extreme minimum temperature is -20 to -10 °F, which is -28.9 to -23.3 °C.

Is zone 5 colder than the zones next to it?

Zone 5 is warmer than zone 4 by 10 degrees Fahrenheit at the range limits.
