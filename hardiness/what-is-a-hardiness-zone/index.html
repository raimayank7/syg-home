What is a plant hardiness zone?

A hardiness zone is a label for how cold the coldest nights of a typical winter get at a place.

The definition

The USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map is based on the average annual extreme minimum winter temperature: for each year, the lowest temperature reached in the winter at that spot, averaged over 30 years. The current (2023) map uses weather data from 1991 to 2020. The temperatures are grouped into 10-degree Fahrenheit zones, numbered from zone 1 (the coldest) to zone 13 (the warmest). Each zone is split into two half zones, "a" and "b", each covering 5 degrees. So zone 7a is the colder half of zone 7 and 7b the warmer half.

ZoneAverage annual extreme minimum
3-40 to -30 °F (-40 to -34.4 °C)
5-20 to -10 °F (-28.9 to -23.3 °C)
70 to 10 °F (-17.8 to -12.2 °C)
920 to 30 °F (-6.7 to -1.1 °C)
1140 to 50 °F (4.4 to 10 °C)
1360 to 70 °F (15.6 to 21.1 °C)

What the number is used for

Nurseries and seed catalogues list a zone, or a range of zones, in the descriptions of perennial plants (plants that live through several winters). If a plant is described as hardy in zones 5 to 9, it is expected to survive the usual winter lows of zone 5 and warmer, and it will also have a limit in the warm direction because of summer heat. The map is a general guide for choosing perennial plants. According to the USDA, the zone colours and numbers make it easy to compare plant cold-hardiness across regions, and gardeners can use them to judge which perennials have done well in other places with the same zone.

What it is not

It is an average of the yearly lowest temperatures, not the lowest temperature that has ever occurred or will occur. A rare cold snap of a day or two can be colder than the zone average, and plants growing at the very cold edge of the zones they are adapted to can be lost in such a year. The zone also says nothing about the length of the growing season, rainfall, summer heat, soil or wind. See zones are not frost dates.

Zones across the US

Most of the 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico fall between zones 3 and 10. In the ZIP code table this site uses, zones 1 and 2 appear only in Alaska, and zones 12 and 13 only in Hawaii and Puerto Rico. Each zone page on this site shows the split. The zones changed in 2012 and again in 2023 as the weather data were updated, and the USDA notes that a changed zone does not mean you should remove plants that have thrived in your garden.

Source: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, "How to Use the Maps" (planthardiness.ars.usda.gov), read on 13 November 2026.

Is a bigger zone number warmer?

Yes. Zone 1 is the coldest and zone 13 the warmest.

Do hardiness zones apply outside the US?

The USDA map covers the US and Puerto Rico. Other countries use their own systems; a zone number from another system is not interchangeable.
