Hardiness Zones

All hardiness zones, 1 to 13

Winter minimum temperatures and ZIP code counts.

The USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map divides the country into 13 zones, each covering a 10 degree Fahrenheit band of the average annual extreme minimum winter temperature. Zone 1 is the coldest and zone 13 the warmest, and each zone has two 5-degree halves, a and b. This table lists all 13 with the Celsius equivalent and the number of ZIP codes in the 2023 table. Choose a zone to see its half zones, its regions and what it means for plants.

How to read the zones

A zone number is only about winter cold. It tells you the average lowest temperature of the year, not how hot the summer is, how much rain falls or how long the growing season is. A plant label that says “hardy in zones 5 to 9” means that the plant should survive winter in any place whose average coldest night is within that range; it will usually not survive in zone 4 and may struggle in the heat of zone 10. A plant that is hardy to a lower zone number than yours will survive your winters, but one that is only hardy to a higher number than yours may be killed by a cold spell.

The half zones, a and b, split each zone into two 5 degree Fahrenheit bands: a is the colder half and b is the warmer half. Gardeners in a zone’s colder half should be a little more careful with borderline plants, and mulch or shelter them in winter.

Where most ZIP codes fall

Of the 40,502 ZIP codes in the table, the most common zone is zone 6 with 10,458 ZIP codes (26%), followed by zone 7 (8,732, 22%) and zone 8 (6,809, 17%). The warmest and coldest zones cover very few: zone 13 has 128 ZIP codes and zone 1 has 16. If you are buying plants, remember that most nursery stock is grown and labelled for the middle zones, so gardeners in the coldest and warmest places need to look more carefully at the label.
