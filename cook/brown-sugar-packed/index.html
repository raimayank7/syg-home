Brown sugar, packed: cups, spoons and grams

Weight of a cup, a tablespoon and a teaspoon, and how many cups are in 100 g to 1 kg.

One US cup of brown sugar weighs about 220 g (7.8 oz), according to the USDA household-measure record for "Sugars, brown" (measured as: packed). A metric 250 ml cup holds a little more, about 232 g. That makes it 93% the weight of the same cup of water (about 237 g).

The USDA gives more than one cup weight for this ingredient, because the weight depends on its form. The lightest is a cup of unpacked at 145 g, the heaviest a cup of packed at 220 g: a difference of 52%. Read the recipe to see which form it means, and use the matching figure from the table.

Measuring tip

Sugar and syrups are dense. For syrups, a lightly oiled spoon or cup lets the sweetener slide out, and a cup of syrup is far heavier than a cup of water. Brown sugar is pressed into the cup, white sugar is not.

Questions

How many grams are in a cup of brown sugar?

A US cup of Brown sugar weighs about 220 g. A metric 250 ml cup is about 232 g.

How many cups is 500 g of Brown sugar?

500 g is about 2 and 1/4 cups (2.27 US cups).

How many tablespoons are in 100 g of Brown sugar?

100 g is about 7.3 tablespoons or 21.8 teaspoons, taking a US tablespoon as one sixteenth of a US cup.

Where do these figures come from?

They are household measure weights from the USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy database (record "Sugars, brown"), a public-domain data set. Your own ingredient may differ by a few per cent, so weigh when a recipe needs to be exact.
