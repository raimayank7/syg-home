US, metric and UK cups and spoons

A cup is not the same size everywhere. Here are the sizes, and how much it matters.

The sizes

MeasureUS labelling rule (FDA)Metric cup
Cup240 ml250 ml
Tablespoon15 ml15 ml
Teaspoon5 ml5 ml

For nutrition labelling the US Food and Drug Administration defines a teaspoon as 5 ml, a tablespoon as 15 ml, a cup as 240 ml, 1 fl oz as 30 ml and 1 oz in weight as 28 g (21 CFR 101.9(b)(5)(viii), read on eCFR on 13 November 2026). The traditional US customary cup is defined as 8 US fluid ounces, which is 236.588 ml; the USDA gram weights on this site refer to the customary cup. A metric cup is a quarter of a litre, 250 ml. Other countries and older books use other cup and spoon sizes; we did not read an official source for them, so we do not list them. If a recipe does not say which cup it means, assume nothing and weigh.

What this site uses

The USDA weights are for US customary measures: the US cup of 236.588 ml, the US tablespoon (one sixteenth of a cup) and the US teaspoon (one third of a tablespoon). On every ingredient page we show the US cup in grams and, next to it, the metric 250 ml cup, which is the US figure multiplied by 250 / 236.588 (about 5.7 per cent more). The conversion is exact for the same ingredient and the same packing.

Katori, glass and other vessels

Kitchen vessels such as a katori or a steel glass are not measuring cups and their size depends on the set. If a recipe uses one, measure once what yours holds with water (1 ml of water weighs 1 g), write it down and treat that as your cup. The ingredient pages here let you convert any cup or spoon amount to grams once you know the millilitres.

How much does it matter?

Between the US customary cup (236.6 ml) and the metric cup (250 ml) the difference is under 6 per cent, which is hardly noticeable in dal or rice, but can matter in baking. A cake mixed with a metric cup from a US recipe has 6 per cent more of everything, which usually comes out fine, since the ratios are kept. The problem arises only when you mix cups from different sets within one recipe. Use one cup for everything.

Which to pick

Use the cup the recipe was written for. If it is an American recipe, a US cup (or weigh the ingredients using the US column here). If it comes from Australia, Canada or most of Europe and says "250 ml", use the metric figure. If you have only one set and it says 240 ml, that is a common compromise sold as a US cup, and the difference from 236.6 ml is only 1.4 per cent.

How many millilitres is a cup of water in grams?

Water is 1 g per ml, so a US cup is about 237 g and a metric cup 250 g.

Is a cup of tea the same as a cup in a recipe?

No. A teacup is usually 150 to 200 ml.
