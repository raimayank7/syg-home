Weighing or measuring cups: which is better?

Cups are quick. Scales are exact. This guide says when each is the right tool.

Where cups are fine

For everyday cooking, cups and spoons give results that are close enough. Rice and water for a pulao, dal and water, a cup of chopped onion in a curry, a spoonful of ghee: if the amount is off by 10 per cent, the dish is still good, and you adjust by taste. In these cases the weight figures on this site are a convenience, for example to buy the right amount (how many cups is 500 g of masoor dal?) rather than a precision matter.

Where weighing wins

  • Baking cakes, biscuits and pastry. The ratio of flour, fat, sugar and eggs decides the texture. A cup of flour that varies from one measuring to the next can give a dry cake one day and a sticky one the next.
  • Bread. Bakers weigh flour and water, and the water is often expressed as a percentage of the flour weight.
  • Sweets and confectionery. Sugar syrups depend on exact sugar-to-water ratios; barfi and halwa are less forgiving than they look.
  • Dense or sticky ingredients such as honey, ghee, peanut butter and condensed milk, which cling to the cup. A scale takes the weight and none sticks.
  • Anything you want to scale up or down. Grams multiply by any factor with no fractions of a cup to deal with.

Spices are in between

A teaspoon of ground cumin and a teaspoon of salt weigh very differently (about 2 g and 6 g for the USDA records), but spices are used in tiny quantities and taste decides. Use a level spoon, and for fiery chillies add less and taste before adding more. A scale accurate to a gram will not help much with a pinch.

Converting a cup recipe to grams, step by step

  1. List each ingredient and look up its cup weight on this site.
  2. Multiply the cups by the grams per cup.
  3. Write the gram amounts next to the recipe.
  4. Cook the recipe once by weight and note anything that needed adjusting. Keep the annotated recipe.

For example, a recipe asking for 2 cups of maida (about 250 g), 1 cup of sugar (200 g) and half a cup of butter (113 g) becomes 250 g, 200 g and 113 g, and you never need the cup again.

Buying a scale

A digital scale with a tare button, a capacity of about 5 kg and a reading step of 1 g is enough for any home kitchen. Test it by weighing a bottle of water: a litre should read about 1,000 g. Keep it dry and replace the battery when the numbers drift.

Is a cup of water 250 g?

A US cup of water is about 237 g and a metric cup 250 g.

Do I need a scale for Indian cooking?

Not for most daily dishes, but it helps for sweets and baking.
