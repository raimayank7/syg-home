How to measure flour by the cup

Flour is the ingredient most often measured wrong. Here is the method that gives a consistent cup.

The spoon-and-level method

  1. Fluff the flour in its container with a fork or whisk. Flour settles and compacts in the bin, and fluffing breaks that up.
  2. Spoon it into the cup, a spoonful at a time, until it is heaped over the top. Do not shake or tap the cup, and do not press it down.
  3. Level the top with the flat back of a knife, sweeping across the rim.
  4. Tip it into the bowl.

This gives about 125 grams for a cup of white flour, which is the figure most recipe writers assume (the USDA's household measure for plain flour is 125 g). If you scoop with the cup itself, the flour compresses and the cup weighs more than the USDA figure, so the dough can come out dry.

Sifted or not?

The wording of a recipe tells you the order. "1 cup sifted flour" means you sift first and then measure, which gives a lighter cup. "1 cup flour, sifted" means you measure first and then sift, which gives the normal weight. Most modern recipes skip sifting for ordinary flour and ask you to whisk the dry ingredients together instead, which mixes them well enough. For cocoa, icing sugar and besan, which clump, sifting is still worth doing.

Different flours, different cups

The weight of a cup of flour depends on the grind and the grain. In the USDA records a cup of besan is only about 92 g, a cup of rye flour about 102 g, a cup of whole wheat atta about 120 g and a cup of rice flour about 158 g, which is a big spread. A recipe written for one flour cannot be converted to another by cups alone. If you swap atta for maida, replace by weight and expect to adjust the water, because wholewheat flour absorbs more.

Why weighing is better

A digital kitchen scale costs little and removes this whole problem. Set the bowl on the scale, press the tare button to zero it, and add flour until the display shows the number of grams. It is also faster and leaves fewer dishes. The ingredient pages of this site give the weight of each cup so you can convert any cup recipe to grams once and write the result in the margin.

Other tips

  • Store flour in an airtight tin; it takes up moisture in the monsoon, and damp flour weighs more.
  • Use the same cup for all dry ingredients in a recipe.
  • If a dough feels too wet, add flour a spoonful at a time instead of trusting the recipe's quantity, since flours absorb water differently.
Can I use a glass for measuring flour?

Only if you have measured what the glass holds. Use a proper cup or, better, a scale.

Does the humidity change the cup?

Yes, a little: flour that has absorbed moisture is heavier and clumpier.
