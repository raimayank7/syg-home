Oven to air fryer converter

A starting point you can adjust, to be checked with a thermometer.

What the converter does

Type the temperature and time from an oven recipe and the converter shows an air fryer starting point. The adjustment is a rule of thumb that cooks commonly use: a lower temperature and a shorter time, because an air fryer circulates hot air quickly around a small space. This rule is an assumption, not an official figure. We did not find an official conversion, so the two adjustment boxes (temperature reduction and time reduction) start at values you can change. The defaults are 20 °C lower and 20 per cent shorter.

How the arithmetic works

  • Air fryer temperature = oven temperature - temperature reduction.
  • Air fryer time = oven time x (1 - time reduction / 100).

For an oven recipe of 200 °C for 30 minutes, the defaults give 180 °C for 24 minutes. The Fahrenheit equivalent of 180 °C is 356 °F (x 9/5 + 32).

Always check the food, not the clock

Because the conversion is only a starting point, check the food with a thermometer. The official minimum internal temperatures (for example 165 °F or 73.9 °C for all poultry, 145 °F or 62.8 °C for fish) are on the safe cooking temperatures page. Cook until the thermometer reads at least the minimum, even if the converted time has passed, and do not stop early because the time is up. The basket size matters too: a crowded basket takes longer, so cook in batches rather than shortening the check.

Limits

The converter does not know your appliance, the food or how full the basket is. It does not apply to recipes that use steam, a lid or a water bath. Check your appliance's leaflet, and use the pack instructions for frozen foods.

Why 20 degrees and 20 per cent?

They are common rules of thumb used by home cooks, not official values, which is why they are boxes you can change.

Does the converter know if the food is safe?

No. Only a food thermometer reading compared with the official minimum tells you that.
