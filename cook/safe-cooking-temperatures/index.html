Safe cooking temperatures

The official minimum internal temperatures, in Fahrenheit and Celsius.

The official chart

The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) publishes a Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart. It says to cook all food to these minimum internal temperatures, measured with a food thermometer, before removing the food from the heat source, and that for personal preference consumers may cook to higher temperatures. The page was last updated by FSIS on 14 April 2025 and was read for this site on 13 November 2026.

Celsius values are converted from the Fahrenheit figures (subtract 32, multiply by 5, divide by 9) and rounded to one decimal; FSIS prints Celsius for some entries (for example 62.8 °C for 145 °F and 73.9 °C for 165 °F), which match. The chart has no separate air fryer figures: these minimums apply however the food is cooked.

The four guidelines FSIS gives

FSIS says that in every step of food preparation you should follow four guidelines to keep food safe, because you cannot see, smell or taste harmful bacteria: clean (wash hands and surfaces often), separate (keep raw meat apart from other foods), cook (to the right temperature) and chill (refrigerate food promptly).

Using a thermometer with an air fryer

Cooking times printed in recipes and on appliance leaflets are guides: the food, its size, how much is in the basket and the appliance all change the time. The official test is the temperature, not the clock. Insert a food thermometer into the thickest part of the food, away from bone, and compare the reading with the chart. If it is below the minimum, cook a little longer and check again. This site gives no official cooking times for any food, because we did not find an official source for air fryer times.

What this chart does not cover

It is the FSIS chart for the foods it lists. It does not list vegetables, bread or frozen convenience foods (follow the pack instructions), and it does not replace the instructions of your appliance or the advice of local food safety authorities in your country. The chart is American; for India, check the guidance of FSSAI where it differs. This site is independent and not connected with the USDA.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, "Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart", fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart, last updated 14 April 2025, read 13 November 2026.

Is 165 °F the same as 74 °C?

FSIS gives 73.9 °C for 165 °F. Rounded to a whole number that is 74 °C.

Can I cook to a higher temperature?

Yes. FSIS says consumers may choose higher temperatures for reasons of personal preference.

Does the chart say how long to cook?

No. It gives minimum internal temperatures measured with a food thermometer, not cooking times.
