Spices and seasonings: cups to grams

19 ingredients, lightest to heaviest.

Spices are measured in teaspoons and tablespoons, and the weight per spoon depends on how finely they are ground and how heavily the spoon is heaped. Always level the spoon, and use a fresh spice for the stated flavour. This page lists every ingredient in the group, lightest first, with the weight of a US cup, a tablespoon and a teaspoon. Open any ingredient for the full table of fractions of a cup, the grams-to-cups table for 50 g to 1 kg and notes on how to measure it.

The lightest in this group is Oregano, dried at 48 g a cup and the heaviest is Salt, table at 292 g, so a cup of the heaviest weighs about 6.1 times as much as a cup of the lightest. That ratio is the reason recipes that give only cups can go wrong when the wrong ingredient is swapped in by volume.

An asterisk (*) means the USDA gives only a spoon weight and the cup figure is worked out from it.

What this means in the kitchen

Take two ingredients from this group, Oregano, dried and Salt, table. One cup of Oregano, dried weighs about 48 g and one cup of Salt, table about 292 g. If a recipe asks for 500 g, that is about 10.4 cups of Oregano, dried but only about 1.7 cups of Salt, table, so the same weight fills a different number of cups. Half a cup of Oregano, dried is about 24 g and half a cup of Salt, table about 146 g, a difference of 122 g in a single half cup. A recipe that gives only cups assumes you scoop in a particular way, which is why a kitchen scale gives better results for baking, where a few grams change the texture.

When you swap one ingredient for another by volume, check the weights on their pages first. If the two weigh differently per cup, convert to grams, swap by weight, and then measure again in cups if you need to.
