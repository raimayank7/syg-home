Milk, eggs and dairy: cups to grams

15 ingredients, lightest to heaviest.

Liquids such as milk and cream weigh a little more than water, and thick products such as curd, cream cheese and cheese depend on how packed they are. Stir curd before measuring, and weigh cheese when a recipe is sensitive. This page lists every ingredient in the group, lightest first, with the weight of a US cup, a tablespoon and a teaspoon. Open any ingredient for the full table of fractions of a cup, the grams-to-cups table for 50 g to 1 kg and notes on how to measure it.

The lightest in this group is Parmesan, grated at 100 g a cup and the heaviest is Condensed milk, sweetened at 306 g, so a cup of the heaviest weighs about 3.1 times as much as a cup of the lightest. That ratio is the reason recipes that give only cups can go wrong when the wrong ingredient is swapped in by volume.

An asterisk (*) means the USDA gives only a spoon weight and the cup figure is worked out from it.

What this means in the kitchen

Take two ingredients from this group, Parmesan, grated and Condensed milk, sweetened. One cup of Parmesan, grated weighs about 100 g and one cup of Condensed milk, sweetened about 306 g. If a recipe asks for 500 g, that is about 5 cups of Parmesan, grated but only about 1.6 cups of Condensed milk, sweetened, so the same weight fills a different number of cups. Half a cup of Parmesan, grated is about 50 g and half a cup of Condensed milk, sweetened about 153 g, a difference of 103 g in a single half cup. A recipe that gives only cups assumes you scoop in a particular way, which is why a kitchen scale gives better results for baking, where a few grams change the texture.

When you swap one ingredient for another by volume, check the weights on their pages first. If the two weigh differently per cup, convert to grams, swap by weight, and then measure again in cups if you need to.
