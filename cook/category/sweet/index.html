Sugar and sweeteners: cups to grams

7 ingredients, lightest to heaviest.

Sugar and syrups are dense. For syrups, a lightly oiled spoon or cup lets the sweetener slide out, and a cup of syrup is far heavier than a cup of water. Brown sugar is pressed into the cup, white sugar is not. This page lists every ingredient in the group, lightest first, with the weight of a US cup, a tablespoon and a teaspoon. Open any ingredient for the full table of fractions of a cup, the grams-to-cups table for 50 g to 1 kg and notes on how to measure it.

The lightest in this group is Sugar, powdered at 120 g a cup and the heaviest is Corn syrup, light at 341 g, so a cup of the heaviest weighs about 2.8 times as much as a cup of the lightest. That ratio is the reason recipes that give only cups can go wrong when the wrong ingredient is swapped in by volume.

An asterisk (*) means the USDA gives only a spoon weight and the cup figure is worked out from it.
