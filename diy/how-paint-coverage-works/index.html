How paint coverage works

The number on the tin is a lab figure for one coat on a smooth, primed surface. Your walls are different.

What the number means

A tin of emulsion says something like "up to 12 m2 per litre per coat" or "120 sq ft per litre". The makers measure it by spreading paint at a controlled thickness on a smooth, sealed test surface. In practice, paint stretches that far only on a smooth wall that has been primed or already painted, with the right tools and a careful hand. The word *up to* is doing a lot of work.

Coverage comes from how thick a layer of paint stays on the wall. A thin layer of paint contains less of the solid pigment and binder that hide the old colour, so spreading it farther does not help: you simply need another coat. That is why a well-made paint can cover less per litre but need fewer coats. Look at the cost per square metre per *finished* result, not per tin.

What reduces coverage

  • Bare new plaster soaks up paint like a sponge. The first coat is normally thinned with water, as the tin says, or replaced by a primer, and does not count in the coverage figure.
  • Rough or textured walls. There is more surface than the flat area suggests, and the paint fills the dips.
  • Dark old colours covered with light ones, or strong colours covered with anything. Expect an extra coat.
  • Spraying loses paint in the air and on the floor, so allow 20 to 30 per cent more.
  • Thick application. Overloading the roller covers less area, runs and takes longer to dry.
  • Cheap rollers. A short-pile roller on a rough wall leaves gaps; a long pile on a smooth wall splatters.

How to adjust your estimate

The paint calculator has a coverage box in square metres per litre. If you know nothing else, use 10, which is cautious. For a rough wall or bare plaster use 7 to 8. If your tin says 12 and the wall is smooth and already painted a similar colour, use 11. When in doubt, compare: 100 square metres of wall at 10 m2/L for two coats is 20 litres; at 7 it is 28.6 litres. The difference is a full extra tin, and it is cheaper to buy the tin than to go back with a mismatch.

Test patch

Paint a patch of about one square metre with your chosen brand and colour, in a corner behind where furniture will stand. Note how much paint it took and divide. That measured coverage is the figure to enter. A test patch also shows whether the colour is as you expected once it dries, which can be a shade different from the wet paint.

Is coverage the same for ceiling paint?

Ceiling paint is normally thicker and is rated separately. Read that tin.

Does a second coat use as much as the first?

Usually less, because the first coat has already filled the pores of the wall. The calculator assumes the same for each coat, which is cautious.

Why does the paint seem to cover less on the shop's sample card?

Sample cards are printed or painted on smooth board. Your wall rarely is.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
