Planning a concrete slab

Good concrete work is mostly in the preparation. This guide covers a small, non-structural slab.

Scope

This is for light jobs such as paths, patios, shed bases and steps. Anything that carries a vehicle, a wall or a roof needs a design by a qualified engineer. The quantities in the concrete calculator are only as right as the dimensions you feed it.

Planning

  1. Mark the area with pegs and string, and check it is square by comparing the two diagonals; they should be equal.
  2. Decide the fall. Outdoor slabs should slope slightly away from buildings, commonly about 1 in 80, so rainwater drains off. Peg the string at the right height.
  3. Dig out the topsoil and soft ground, deep enough for the sub-base and the slab together.
  4. Lay a sub-base of compacted crushed stone or hardcore, often 75 to 100 mm for light uses, on firm ground. Compact it in layers with a plate compactor or a heavy tamper.
  5. Build formwork of straight timber, staked firmly so it does not bulge. Set the top at the finished level.
  6. Add a damp-proof sheet under a slab for a shed or room, if the ground is damp.
  7. Add reinforcement if the design needs it: mesh held up on small blocks so it sits in the middle, not on the ground.

Thickness

Common thicknesses for light uses are 75 to 100 mm for a path, 100 mm for a patio or shed base and more for anything that takes vehicles. These are general rules and not a design. Use the thickness your plans or your builder give you; the calculator accepts any.

Pouring and finishing

Wet the sub-base lightly. Pour from one end, spread with a shovel, tamp or screed with a straight board across the formwork, and compact to remove air pockets. Wait until the water sheen on the surface has gone, then float it smooth. A broom finish gives grip outdoors. Cut or form control joints, about every 2 to 3 metres on a path or patio, so shrinkage cracks are guided along the joints.

Curing

Keep the slab damp for at least a week, covered with wet sacks or plastic. In hot weather, start at once and keep it shaded; in frost, cover it with insulation. Remove the formwork after a day or two, and do not load the slab for a week or more.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the sub-base. The slab then sinks and cracks.
  • Adding too much water. See concrete mixes explained.
  • Under-ordering materials. Run out half-way and you will have a cold joint, a weak line. The calculator adds 5 per cent for a reason.
  • Pouring in the heat of midday without shade.
Do I need a permit for a slab?

Rules differ by place. A slab for a building or a boundary is more likely to need one. Check with the local authority.

Can I use ready-mix?

For anything above a small area, it is easier and more consistent. The calculator's wet volume is the amount to order.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
