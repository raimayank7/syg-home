Wallpaper pattern repeat and matching

A large pattern looks lovely and costs more paper. The repeat is why.

What a repeat is

A printed design repeats at regular intervals down the roll. The pattern repeat is the distance from one point in the pattern to the same point in the next one, usually printed on the label in centimetres. A plain or textured paper has no repeat (0). A small pattern may repeat every 5 to 10 cm, and a large one can repeat every 60 cm or more.

Each strip you hang has to line up with the one next to it, so you cannot start every strip at the same place on the roll. Each strip is cut a little longer than the wall height so that the pattern can be moved up or down until it matches. In effect you lose up to one repeat on every strip.

Types of match

  • Straight match (or "free match"): the pattern lines up straight across at the same height on every strip. Each strip is the wall height plus up to one repeat.
  • Drop match (or half-drop): the pattern on one strip lines up with the neighbour dropped by half a repeat. You are working at an offset, and the waste per strip is the same as straight match or slightly lower.
  • Random match: the pattern does not need to match, such as with a texture or irregular design. Waste is the least; you only add trimming.

The label gives the type with a symbol. If it is not clear, ask the shop or the maker.

What it does to the count

Each strip of the room is cut at the wall height plus the repeat plus about 10 cm for trimming. The number of strips from one roll is the roll length divided by that cut length, rounded down. For a 10.05 m roll and a 2.5 m wall:

An extra bit of repeat does nothing until it pushes you across a threshold, and then it costs five rolls. That is why the calculator works it with whole strips, and not as an area. For a patterned paper it can be worth checking whether a slightly different roll size gives a better result.

Hanging notes

Hang the first strip from a plumb line and not from the corner, because corners are rarely straight. Paste the wall or the paper as the label says, allow the paper to rest for the time stated so it relaxes, and smooth it out from the centre. Match the pattern at eye level, which is where it is most visible. Work away from the window so that the joins are less obvious.

Ordering

Buy all rolls from one batch; the batch number is printed on the label. If you must reorder, give the batch number to the shop. Keep one spare roll if the room is important; a repair years later needs the same print.

Can I save paper by turning alternate strips upside-down?

Only for a design that is made to hang like that (reversible). Most are not.

Do I need to deduct windows?

Count whole strips over and under windows. Only a really wide opening lets you remove strips.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra.
