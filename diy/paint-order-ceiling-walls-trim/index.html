In what order to paint a room

Paint falls, so work from the top down. Here is the whole job in order.

Why the order matters

Gravity decides: every drip, splatter and roller spray lands below the brush. If you paint the walls first and then the ceiling, you will have to touch up the walls. If you paint the floor first, you will have to cover it. So the general order is ceiling, then walls, then woodwork, then floor, with preparation before all of it.

Preparation (about half the work)

  1. Empty the room or push the furniture to the middle and cover it with sheets. Cover the floor with a dust sheet or old newspaper taped down.
  2. Take down switch plates, curtain rails and picture hooks. Cover sockets and switches with masking tape if you cannot remove the plates safely; switch off the power at the main board before taking plates off.
  3. Repair: fill cracks and holes with filler, let it dry, sand smooth. Scrape any flaking paint.
  4. Clean: wipe dust and grease, especially in a kitchen. Paint does not stick to dust.
  5. Prime any bare plaster, stained patches and filler. Let the primer dry as the tin says.

The painting order

  1. Ceiling. Cut in along the edges with a brush first, then roll. Work in strips away from the window so the light does not show roller joins. Two coats, each allowed to dry.
  2. Walls. Cut in at the corners, edges and around sockets, then roll the field before the cut-in dries. This keeps a "wet edge" and avoids visible joins. Paint one wall at a time from top to bottom, working in sections about a metre wide.
  3. Woodwork. Doors, window frames and skirting boards are painted after the walls so the gloss or enamel is not splashed. Sand lightly, then prime and paint.
  4. Floor edges and touch-ups last. Remove the masking tape while the final coat is slightly tacky, not fully dry, to get a clean edge.

Timing

Allow a full day for preparing a bedroom, a day for the first coat on ceiling and walls and a second day for the second coat; woodwork needs another day because of the drying time between primer, undercoat and gloss. The tin gives the time before recoating, commonly two to four hours for emulsion in warm weather and longer in cold or humid conditions. Open windows to help the paint dry but avoid direct sun and drafts that make it dry unevenly.

Quantities

Work out ceiling, walls and woodwork separately because they use different paints. The paint calculator does the ceiling and walls together, with a tick for the ceiling; run it twice if the ceiling is a different paint.

Should I paint the ceiling the same day as the walls?

You can if the ceiling coat is dry to the touch, normally after a couple of hours. Otherwise, wait until the next day.

Can I roll over masking tape?

Take it off before the paint dries hard, or the film cracks and lifts paint with it.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
