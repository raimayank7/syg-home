How much waste to allow when buying tiles

Ten per cent is the usual answer, but it is not the right answer for every room.

Where the waste goes

Waste is not only broken tiles. Most of it is cuts: at each wall the last tile in a row must be cut to fit, and the piece that is cut off is rarely used elsewhere. Added to that are tiles that arrive damaged, tiles that crack while being cut and a few spares you should keep for repairs. The more edges a room has compared with its floor area, the more waste.

Rules of thumb

LayoutTypical allowance
Straight lay, simple rectangular room5 to 10%
Straight lay, small room or many corners10 to 12%
Large tiles (600 mm and over)add 2 to 5%
Diagonal (45 degree) layout15%
Herringbone and complex patterns15 to 20%
Natural stone, hand-made tiles10 to 15%

These are common allowances used by tilers, not exact rules. A skilled tiler who plans where cuts fall can work with less; a first-time tiler should use more.

Why bigger tiles waste more

A 600 mm tile cut at a wall leaves a piece that is probably too small for the next row. With 300 mm tiles, the offcut from one row often fits at the start of another. The wall at the end of a long row is the same, but the loss is smaller. Large tiles also break more often, because they flex when you carry them and when they are laid on an uneven floor.

Worked example

You need to tile a bathroom floor of 6 square metres with 300 x 300 mm tiles laid diagonally. The area of one tile with a 3 mm joint is 0.0918 m2 (0.303 x 0.303), so the tiles with no waste are 6 / 0.0918 = 65.3. With 15 per cent that is 75.1, rounded up to 76 tiles. For a straight lay with 10 per cent, 72 tiles are needed. The diagonal costs four extra tiles in this room, but the percentage matters more in a big room.

Spare tiles

Keep a box (or at least a few tiles) after finishing. Tiles go out of production; matching the shade later is hard because every batch differs. Keep them with the batch number noted. The cost of two spare tiles is small compared to a visible repair.

Reducing waste

  • Dry-lay the first row without adhesive, adjusting so that the cut tiles at either end are more than half a tile wide.
  • Plan the layout so that the cut edges fall under cupboards or against walls where they are hidden.
  • Use offcuts for small areas such as under a radiator or around a pipe.
  • Buy one extra box instead of ordering again; delivery may bring a different batch.
Does the waste percentage apply to wall tiles too?

Yes. Walls with sockets, windows and niches have many cuts, so use 10 to 15%.

Is it cheaper to buy exactly what the calculator says?

Only if nothing goes wrong. The reserve costs a little; a second order costs more and may not match.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra.
