Plaster thickness and mix

Plaster is a layer of mortar. How thick it is and how rich the mix is decides how long it lasts.

Typical thicknesses

SurfaceCommon thickness
Internal wallabout 12 mm
External wall15 to 20 mm
Ceiling6 to 10 mm
Floor screed25 to 50 mm

These are the thicknesses commonly used in practice; the project specification decides, and local codes may differ. Plaster thinner than 6 mm on masonry is hard to apply evenly and does not cover the irregularities of the wall. Plaster thicker than about 20 mm in one coat is heavy, sags, and cracks as it dries.

Coats

A wall that needs 20 mm or more is plastered in two coats: a rough first coat (the scratch coat) is pressed on, left to firm up and scratched to give grip, and the second coat is applied smooth. External plaster over a mixed surface of brick and concrete sometimes has wire mesh across the joint between the two, because the two materials move differently and a crack would otherwise open there.

Mix

A richer mix (1:4, one cement to four sand) is stronger and more water-resistant. A leaner mix (1:6) is more flexible and cheaper, and suits internal walls on bricks. Using a mix that is too rich on a weak wall cracks the plaster. Use clean, well-graded sand, not too dusty or too coarse. The calculator takes any of the ratios you choose.

Preparing the wall

  1. Clean the surface and remove loose mortar from joints. Rake out the brick joints to 10 mm for a better key.
  2. Wet the wall before plastering, especially in hot weather; a dry wall pulls the water out of the mortar and weakens it.
  3. Fix guide strips (screeds) or lines at the intended thickness to keep the plane flat.
  4. Plaster from the top down in panels, and finish each panel with a straight board.

Curing

Plaster needs to be kept damp for several days after it sets, by sprinkling it with water morning and evening, or it will shrink and craze. Avoid direct sun and wind. Painting follows only when the plaster has dried, which can take weeks; the paint maker states the wait.

Quantity

The wet volume is area x thickness, plus 10 per cent for unevenness. The dry materials are 1.27 times that. A worked example is on the plaster calculator page: 50 m2 at 12 mm in 1:4 mortar needs 5 bags of cement and 0.67 m3 of sand. The thickness is the biggest lever on the quantity: 20 mm instead of 12 mm needs two-thirds more.

Why does plaster crack?

Too rich a mix, too much water, no curing, too thick a coat, or movement in the wall. Often it is a combination.

Can I plaster over old paint?

Remove loose paint and roughen the surface, or use a bonding agent, according to the product's instructions.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra.
