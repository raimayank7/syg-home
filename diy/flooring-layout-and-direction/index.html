Which way to lay a floor

A few decisions before the first plank affect how the floor looks for years.

Planks: lay them toward the light

The common rule is to lay planks along the direction of the main light, which means the joints between planks run toward the window and are less visible. A second rule, particularly for a hall, is to lay along the longest dimension, which makes a narrow room look longer. When the two rules disagree, choose the one that looks better to you, or lay a test row.

Over an existing floor of boards, lay across the old boards, not parallel with them. For a floating floor on a flat concrete base the direction is free.

Where to start

Do not start at a wall by default. Measure the width of the room and divide by the plank width. If the last row would be a sliver narrower than about 50 mm, make the first row narrower as well, so that both edge rows are equal and more than half a plank. The walls are rarely straight, so snap a chalk line to guide the first row.

Stagger the end joints from row to row, by at least 300 mm or according to the maker's instructions. This is stronger and looks better than a regular pattern, and it also uses offcuts efficiently, lowering waste.

The expansion gap

Floating wood, laminate and vinyl planks expand and shrink with temperature and humidity. They are laid with a gap of about 8 to 12 mm around the room (the maker states the exact figure), hidden by skirting boards or a trim. A floor without a gap will buckle in the first damp season. Spacers hold the gap while you lay. The gap goes also at door frames, pipes, and against any fixed object.

Tiles

Tiles are laid from the centre of the room or from the most visible doorway, not from a corner, so that cut tiles at both edges are balanced. Dry-lay a row in each direction to see where the cuts fall. For a diagonal layout, mark a line at 45 degrees from the centre. See grout lines and joint width for the spacing.

Order of work

  1. Finish the walls and paint before the floor so that no paint lands on it, but leave the skirting until after.
  2. Lay the floor, keeping the expansion gap.
  3. Fit the skirting and door thresholds last.
  4. Do not move heavy furniture in until the adhesive or the floor has settled for the time stated.

What it does to the quantity

A diagonal layout wastes more than a straight one; an irregular room wastes more than a square one. The flooring calculator lets you set the waste percentage to suit the layout. For a laminate in a simple room use about 10 per cent, and 15 or more for diagonal.

Should the floor go under the skirting or the skirting go on top?

The gap is hidden under the skirting, which is fitted after the floor. Do not pin the floor down through it.

Do I need underlay?

Floating floors normally use one: it adds sound and moisture protection. The floor's maker states which one.
