Units for cement, sand and aggregate

Orders go wrong when the buyer thinks in cubic metres and the seller thinks in brass or tonnes. These are the conversions.

Cement

Cement is sold in 50 kg bags in India and in many other countries (in the US the common bag is 94 lb, about 42.6 kg, and a bag of premixed concrete is 60 or 80 lb). The calculators treat a 50 kg bag as 0.0347 cubic metres, which is 50 kg at a bulk density of 1,440 kg per cubic metre; many estimators use 1.25 cubic feet.

Sand and aggregate

These are loose bulk materials and are sold by volume or weight, depending on the place:

UnitEqual toWhere used
Cubic metre (m3)35.31 cubic feetStandard metric unit
Cubic foot (cft, cu ft)0.02832 m3India, UK trade, US
Brass100 cubic feet (2.83 m3)Parts of India, especially for sand and aggregate
Cubic yard27 cubic feet (0.7646 m3)US, UK
Tonneabout 0.6 m3 of sandWeight sales

The brass is a trade unit, and its definition (100 cubic feet) is common, but a few regions or suppliers use a different one, so ask a new supplier what they mean by a brass before you order.

Volume against weight

Sand and stone are weighed by tipper and truck weigh bridges, so a supplier may quote by the tonne while you calculate by volume. To convert, you need the density, which changes with the material, moisture and size grading. A rough guide for dry sand is about 1.5 to 1.6 tonnes per cubic metre and for crushed stone aggregate about 1.4 to 1.6. These are ballpark; ask the supplier for the weight of one load. See the gravel and soil calculator where you can choose a material and get both.

Bulking of sand

Damp sand takes up more space than dry sand because the water films push the grains apart. This is called bulking and can make a cubic metre of damp sand contain only about 80 per cent as much sand as a dry one. When mixing by volume, damp sand therefore gives a leaner mix than planned. For important work, the specification will say how to correct for it.

Worked conversion

Your calculator result says 0.92 m3 of sand. In cubic feet that is 0.92 x 35.31 = 32.5 cft, which is about a third of a brass. In tonnes at 1.55 t/m3 it is 1.43 tonnes. Ask for the quantity in the unit the supplier uses and add a little.

Quick conversions

  • 1 m3 = 35.31 cu ft = 1.308 cu yd
  • 1 cu yd = 0.7646 m3
  • 100 cu ft (one brass) = 2.832 m3
  • 1 bag (50 kg) cement = 0.0347 m3 = 1.23 cu ft
Why do different calculators give different cement bags for the same job?

Some use a dry volume factor of 1.52, others 1.54 or 1.57, and bag volumes differ. The ordering allowance covers the difference.

How much does a bag of cement weigh?

50 kg in India. Always read the bag.
