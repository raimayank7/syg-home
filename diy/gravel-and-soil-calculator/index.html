Gravel, soil and mulch calculator

Give the area and the depth you want and pick the material. You get cubic metres, cubic yards, cubic feet and an approximate weight in tonnes.

Volume, then weight

Garden and drive materials are sold by volume (cubic metres or yards) or by weight (tonnes), and suppliers often quote in the other unit than the one you measured in. The page does both. The volume is area x depth: a patch 20 square metres in size covered to 50 mm (0.05 m) is 1.0 m3, plus 5 per cent for settling and uneven ground makes 1.05 m3. In imperial units that is 1.37 cubic yards, or 37 cubic feet.

Weight needs the density of the material. The material box offers typical densities in tonnes per cubic metre: gravel and crushed stone around 1.6, sand about 1.6 (dry sand a little less, wet sand more), topsoil about 1.3, compost and bark about 0.5 to 0.6 and mulch about 0.4. These are typical figures for loose material and they vary a great deal with moisture and the size of the pieces. For the 1.05 m3 of gravel at 1.6, the weight is 1.68 tonnes.

Depth guide

How deep to spread depends on the job; the guide on spreading gravel, soil and mulch goes into the reasons. As rough guides: a mulch layer around plants 50 to 75 mm; a gravel path 50 mm of top layer on a firm base; a driveway needs a compacted base of crushed stone well over 100 mm; a lawn dressing only 10 mm; raised beds the full height of the frame filled with topsoil and compost.

Ordering tips

Ask the supplier what unit they sell by, and what their material weighs per cubic metre or yard: they will know their own product. Lorries deliver by weight or by volume, so a heap of 1.68 tonnes of 20 mm gravel is about a cubic metre. Order a small allowance over, because a bit extra is easy to use but a short load means a second delivery charge. Spread the material as soon as it comes, on a tarp if it is soil, because loose heaps are washed onto the road by rain.

Why compaction matters

Loose material settles by ten per cent or more when it is walked on or rained on; compacted base layers by even more. The waste box lets you set that allowance. Use 10 per cent for soil and mulch that will settle and 5 per cent for gravel on a hard base.

Is the weight accurate?

It is an estimate. Wet material is up to a third heavier than dry. For a load that must not exceed the capacity of a vehicle, ask the supplier.

How do I convert tonnes to cubic metres?

Divide by the density: 1.68 tonnes at 1.6 t/m3 is 1.05 m3.

Does it work for sand under paving?

Yes: choose sand, enter the area and the bedding depth (usually 25 to 50 mm).
