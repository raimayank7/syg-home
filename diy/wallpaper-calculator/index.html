Wallpaper calculator

Enter the total length of wall to be covered, the height and the roll size. Add the pattern repeat if the paper has one.

How rolls are counted

Wallpaper is hung in vertical strips, so the arithmetic is not square metres. The number of strips is the length of wall divided by the width of the roll, rounded up, because a part strip still needs a whole drop. Each strip is cut from the roll at a length of wall height + pattern repeat + trimming allowance. The page uses 10 cm of trimming as a standard allowance. The number of whole strips that can be cut from one roll depends on the roll length, and again you round down, since a short leftover piece is no use. Finally rolls = strips / strips per roll, rounded up.

Worked example

A room has walls totalling 14 m round, the wall height is 2.5 m, the roll is 0.53 m wide and 10.05 m long (a common European size), and the pattern repeat is 32 cm.

StepWorkingResult
Strips14 / 0.53 = 26.4, rounded up27
Length of one cut2.5 + 0.32 + 0.102.92 m
Strips from one roll10.05 / 2.92 = 3.44, rounded down3
Rolls27 / 39

Notice the waste: the 0.44 left over on each roll is lost, plus the repeat. With a plain paper (repeat 0) a roll gives 3.8, so 3 strips again here: the same count, but if the height were 2.4 m a plain paper would give 4 strips per roll and need only 7 rolls. Pattern repeat is the main reason wallpaper estimates vary.

Doors and windows

The page does not take off doors and windows. The usual practice among decorators is to deduct only if an opening is wider than a strip and tall: the strips above and below a window still need full-length cuts from the roll. If a wall has a very large opening, count that wall separately and subtract whole strips.

Matching and batches

Check the batch number printed on each roll; rolls from different batches can differ in shade. Buy one extra roll if the paper is expensive to replace, and keep it for repairs. If a design is printed in a wide roll (for instance 1 m wide), type the true width, and the same method works.

Read more about repeats and how they change the count in wallpaper pattern repeat.

What if the paper has a half-drop repeat?

Use the full repeat length for the safe estimate. Half-drop designs often waste a little less, but the label of the paper gives the exact figure.

Should I include the ceiling?

Not with this calculator. Ceilings are papered in lengths across the room; estimate the area and divide by the roll coverage.

My roll is in feet and inches.

Choose inches or feet for the roll size and metres or feet for the room.
