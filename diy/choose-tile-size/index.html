How to choose a tile size

Size changes the look, the labour and the waste. Here is how the common sizes compare.

Common sizes and where they work

Size (mm)Where it is usedNotes
100 x 100 to 200 x 200Splashbacks, feature walls, small bathroomsMany joints, easy cuts
300 x 300Bathrooms, kitchen floors, stairsForgiving on uneven floors
300 x 600Walls in bathrooms and kitchensLong tiles make walls look taller
450 x 450Living areas, corridorsCommon in older houses
600 x 600Living rooms, large kitchensThe most popular floor size now
800 x 800 and 600 x 1200Open-plan spaces, showroomsNeeds a flat base and skilled laying

What size changes

Look. Large tiles with narrow joints make a room look larger and calmer because there are fewer lines. Small tiles add pattern and texture. In a small room, a large tile can look right if it is not cut into small pieces at every wall; in a tiny bathroom, small tiles may fit the walls better.

Joints and cleaning. More joints means more grout to get dirty; a floor of 300 mm tiles has twice as many metres of joint per square metre as a floor of 600 mm. The grout line is the part of a tiled surface that stains first.

Laying. Large tiles show every unevenness in the floor as a lipped edge. The floor must be flat to within a few millimetres over two metres; for a floor that is not, smaller tiles are more forgiving, or the floor must be levelled with a screed first. Large tiles are also heavy and need two people to handle.

Waste. Large tiles waste more at edges; see how much tile waste to allow.

Slip resistance. A bathroom or outdoor floor needs a tile rated for grip. Polished tiles are slippery when wet. Check the rating the maker gives for wet areas.

A simple decision method

  1. Measure the room and find the smallest wall length.
  2. Try each candidate size: how many whole tiles fit along the length and how big is the cut tile at the end? An end cut below about a quarter of a tile looks poor and breaks easily. Shifting the start line or changing the size fixes it.
  3. Check the weight and the base: upper floors, balconies and old slabs have load limits, and thin boards need a stiff base.
  4. Order a sample, put it down in the room and look at it in daylight and at night.

The tile calculator lets you change the tile size and see the count; run it for two sizes and compare.

Is a bigger tile always better?

No. In a small, awkward room the cuts waste material, and an uneven floor will not take a large tile.

What size is best for a bathroom wall?

Many people use 300 x 600 mm tiles. The best size also depends on the height of the room and where the fittings fall.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
