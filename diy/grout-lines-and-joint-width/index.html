Grout lines and joint width

A joint is not a flaw: it takes up movement, evens out size differences and holds the grout.

Why tiles need a joint

Tiles are never exactly the same size, they expand and shrink slightly with heat and damp, and the base they sit on moves a little. A joint between them absorbs this. A tile laid tight against its neighbour will chip at the edge or lift when the floor moves. The joint is then filled with grout, which keeps dirt out and gives the surface an even look.

How wide

The maker's instructions give the minimum joint for each tile, and that figure wins. As a general guide:

  • Rectified tiles, cut accurately after firing so every edge is square: joints of 1.5 to 3 mm are possible.
  • Ordinary porcelain and ceramic floor tiles: about 3 to 5 mm.
  • Wall tiles: 2 to 3 mm.
  • Handmade, rustic and natural stone: 5 to 10 mm or more, because sizes vary.
  • Outdoor and heated floors: wider, because of larger movement.

The joint is also set by the spacers you use. Cross-shaped plastic spacers come in sizes such as 2, 3 and 5 mm. Use one size across the whole job and take them out before grouting.

How the joint changes the count

The joint changes the size of each tile's cell. With a 600 mm tile, a 3 mm joint makes a cell of 603 mm, which covers 0.3636 m2 rather than 0.36 m2: one per cent fewer tiles. With a 5 mm joint on a 300 mm tile the difference is 3 per cent. Over a big floor it is a few tiles, or one box. Putting your joint into the tile calculator accounts for this.

Grout colour

A grout close to the tile colour makes a calm surface and hides small errors. A contrast colour (white tile, dark grout) shows every line and every wobble in the laying. On floors a mid-grey or a colour close to the tile hides dirt. Grout colours dry lighter than they look wet, and a sample board helps.

Movement joints

Large floors and floors over heating or in sunlight need a flexible joint at the edges and at intervals, filled with a flexible sealant instead of grout. The maker or an experienced tiler can say where. Cracks in tiles over a large floor are often the sign of a missing movement joint.

Can I lay tiles without any joint?

Not recommended. Even rectified tiles are laid with a hairline joint, because no wall or floor is perfectly flat.

How long before I can grout?

Follow the adhesive's instructions; it is commonly a day, or longer in cold or damp weather.

Do I seal the grout?

Cement grout is porous and is often sealed in wet areas; epoxy grout is not. The grout maker states which.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra.
