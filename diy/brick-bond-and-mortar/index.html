Brick bond and mortar

A wall is more than a pile of bricks: the pattern ties it together, and the mortar joint changes the numbers.

Bond

The bond is the pattern in which bricks are laid. A brick has a long face (the *stretcher*) and a short end face (the *header*). The pattern decides how the load spreads through the wall and how strong it is.

  • Stretcher bond: every brick laid lengthwise, each course shifted by half a brick. It makes a wall one half-brick thick (the width of the brick, 90 mm for a 190 x 90 x 90 mm modular brick). Used for partitions and cavity walls.
  • Header bond: every brick laid with its end showing, making a wall one brick long thick. Used for curved walls.
  • English bond: alternate courses of stretchers and headers. It is strong and the traditional way to build a one-brick-thick wall.
  • Flemish bond: stretchers and headers alternate in each course. It looks decorative.

For counting bricks the pattern does not change the numbers per square metre of wall of the same thickness, just that the wall is half a brick or one brick thick. A wall one brick thick has twice the bricks of a half-brick wall.

The mortar joint

The standard joint between bricks is about 10 mm thick, both between the courses (bed joint) and between the bricks in a course (perpend). The joint must be full, not hollow, or the wall is weak and lets water in. Thicker joints use more mortar and make the wall weaker; thinner joints are neater but not forgiving of uneven bricks.

The size of the joint changes the brick count. For a 190 x 90 x 90 brick, a 10 mm joint gives a cell of 200 x 100 mm, so 50 bricks per square metre in a half-brick wall. With a 12 mm joint the cell is 202 x 102 mm, and the count falls to 48.5. Over a 100 m2 wall that is 150 bricks.

The mortar mix

For brickwork the usual mixes are 1:4 to 1:6 cement to sand. The mortar needs to be weaker than the brick, so that if the wall moves, any cracking is in the joint, which can be repaired, not in the brick. Plastering sand is finer than building sand; use the one for the job.

Mortar quantity

The brick calculator finds the mortar volume as the wall volume minus the volume of the bricks, then applies the dry volume factor. For a 10 m x 3 m half-brick wall: wall 2.7 m3, bricks 2.31 m3, mortar 0.39 m3 wet. That is three bags of cement at 1:6. The mortar for a one-brick-thick wall is proportionally higher, because there are two layers of joints in each course.

Practical points

  • Soak bricks in water before laying, in dry weather; dry bricks suck water out of the mortar.
  • Check the wall with a spirit level each course, and use a line to keep the courses straight.
  • A common rule of thumb is not to build more than about 1.2 m in a day: the mortar below needs time to harden.
  • Leave the joints slightly raked back if the wall will be plastered, to key the plaster.
Which bond is best for a boundary wall?

English or Flemish bond, one brick thick or more, with piers as the design says; an engineer decides for tall walls.

How many bricks in 100 square feet of wall?

For a half-brick wall of modular bricks and 10 mm joints, 100 sq ft is 9.29 m2, so about 465 bricks.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
