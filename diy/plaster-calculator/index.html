Plaster and screed calculator

Type the area and the thickness in millimetres, choose a mix, and you get cement bags and sand.

The method

Plaster and floor screed are both mortar spread in a thin layer. The wet volume is the area multiplied by the thickness: 50 square metres at 12 mm (0.012 m) is 0.60 m3. The page adds 10 per cent, because walls are never perfectly flat and a thickness varies where it fills a dip, bringing the wet volume to 0.66 m3. Dry materials are taken as about 1.27 times that (an estimating assumption, editable in the calculator), here 0.84 m3, and they split in the mix ratio. In a 1:4 mix that is one part cement and four sand: 0.168 m3 of cement is 5 bags and 0.67 m3 of sand.

Choosing the thickness and the ratio

Internal wall plaster is commonly about 12 mm thick, external plaster 15 to 20 mm, and a ceiling plaster thinner, around 6 to 10 mm. Floor screeds are usually 25 to 50 mm. Never apply an unusually thick coat in one go: thick plaster sags and cracks, so builders put it on in two coats when the total is over about 20 mm. The mix is usually 1:4 or 1:5 for external and 1:6 for internal plaster, with stronger mixes for floors; the mason or the engineer's specification decides.

Worked example

You are plastering a room with 50 square metres of internal wall at 12 mm in 1:4 mortar.

Wet mortar50 x 0.012 x 1.100.66 m3
Dry materials0.66 x 1.270.84 m3
Cement0.84 x 1/5 / 0.03475 bags
Sand0.84 x 4/50.67 m3 (24 cu ft)

Getting the area

The area is the surface of the walls, which for a plain room is the same wall area as in the paint calculator: the perimeter times the height, less large openings. Openings with deep reveals still need plastering inside the reveal, so some estimators do not deduct small windows and doors at all. Add the ceiling separately if it is being plastered.

Sand

Fine sand is used for plaster, coarser sand for masonry. Sand is sold in cubic feet, brass or tonnes depending on where you live, and damp sand takes up more volume than dry sand, so a supplier may ask you to order a little more in the monsoon. See units for cement, sand and aggregate.

Does the calculator include a cement finish or putty?

No. Wall putty is a separate product sold by the bag with its own coverage.

What mix is best for plaster?

There is no single best. A richer mix is stronger but cracks more; a leaner one is weaker but flexible. Follow the specification for the job.

How can I reduce waste?

Plaster in sections, mix only what can be used within the time the mortar stays workable, and keep the sand covered so rain does not wash it away.

Estimates only. Real jobs differ with the surface, the product and the skill of the worker. Read the maker's instructions on the tin, bag or box, and order a little extra. Structural work (columns, beams, roof slabs, retaining walls) must be designed by a qualified engineer. This is an independent site.
