Spreading gravel, soil and mulch

The right depth keeps weeds down, drains well and does not waste money. Here are the usual depths and the reasons.

Gravel for paths and drives

A gravel path needs three things: a firm base, a weed barrier and a top layer. A path for walking: remove the topsoil, lay a geotextile fabric to stop weeds, add 50 to 75 mm of compacted crushed stone as a base, and top it with 25 to 50 mm of gravel. A drive carrying cars needs a deeper, well-compacted base of crushed stone, designed for the load. Edging of timber, metal or brick keeps the gravel from spreading.

Angular crushed stone locks together and stays put under foot, while rounded pea gravel rolls and is better for decoration than for a path. Fine material mixed in among the larger stones makes the surface compact and firm.

Topsoil

Spread 100 to 150 mm of topsoil for a new lawn on poor ground and around 150 to 300 mm for a flower or vegetable bed. For a thin lawn dressing, 10 mm of sandy topsoil is enough. Break up the sub-soil under the new topsoil, so roots and water can pass. Raised beds are filled to the height of the frame. Soil settles, so fill slightly above the final level.

Mulch

Mulch is any covering over soil (bark, wood chips, compost, straw, gravel). It holds moisture, keeps down weeds and protects roots from heat and frost. A layer of 50 to 75 mm is normal; deeper mulch can rot stems if it piles against them, so keep it a hand's width clear of tree trunks and plant stems. Fresh wood chips take nitrogen from the soil while they rot; use them on paths and around established shrubs, not where seedlings will grow.

Preparing the ground

  1. Clear weeds and roots; a thick weed needs digging out, not covering.
  2. Level and slope the ground away from walls.
  3. Water the soil if it is dry.
  4. Lay the fabric or the base before the top layer.

Working out the quantity

Measure the length and width (or split an odd shape into parts) and the depth. The gravel and soil calculator multiplies for the volume, adds an allowance for settling and gives tonnes for the material you select. For a patch of 20 m2 at 50 mm that is 1.05 m3 with 5 per cent extra, and about 1.7 tonnes of gravel.

Ordering

Ask whether the supplier sells by the tonne or the cubic metre; ask how big their bags or loads are. Bulk is cheaper than bags but needs a place to be tipped, on a hard surface or a sheet. Check that the material is the size you want: "20 mm" gravel and "10 mm" gravel look and feel different.

How much does a cubic metre of gravel cover?

At 50 mm deep, 20 square metres; at 25 mm, 40 square metres.

Do I need a membrane under gravel?

It helps to stop weeds and to keep gravel from sinking into soil, but it is not essential on a well-compacted base.
