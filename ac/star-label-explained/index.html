The BEE star label for room air conditioners

The label gives a star level and the ISEER behind it. Here is what BEE says about how it is measured.

What the label shows

The BEE Appliance Labeling and Compliance Regulations, 2026 (Gazette of India, 26 December 2025) list the particulars of a label: the name of the appliance, the brand, the model number, the energy performance parameter, the label series, the rated capacity or output, the star level, the country of origin and the BEE logo, plus anything else required for the appliance. They define the star level as the level of energy performance shown on the label based on the notification issued under section 14(a) of the Energy Conservation Act. Room air conditioners are in Schedule A, the list of appliances for which labelling is mandatory.

How ISEER is tested

The room air conditioner notification defines ISEER as the ratio of the total annual heat the equipment may remove from indoor air in cooling to the total annual energy it consumes (see the running cost calculator). It says the methodology follows ISO 16358 with deviations for Indian conditions: a bin temperature range of 24 to 43 degrees Celsius and 1,600 cooling hours per annum, with an outdoor temperature distribution table (for example 527 hours at 24 degrees and 660 hours at 27 degrees). Test conditions are an outdoor 35 degrees Celsius dry bulb and an indoor 27 degrees dry bulb. Units are tested under IS 1391 (Part 1 for unitary, Part 2 for split). The notification also states there is no negative tolerance on star levels, and that the star level plan shall be reviewed every two years.

Star bands, by period

The notifications print ISEER bands for unitary (window) type and split type air conditioners, in tables that apply to the year of manufacture, purchase or sale. How the 2026 bands came about. S.O. 2794(E) of 16 July 2024 extended the period of tables (f) to 31 December 2025 and inserted tables 3.1(g) and 3.2(g) for 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2028. S.O. 3984(E) of 28 August 2025 then changed the end of table 3.1(g) from 2028 to 2029, shortened table 3.2(g) so that it ends in 2027, and inserted table 3.2(h) for split air conditioners for 1 January 2028 to 31 December 2029. The same amendment says that 1 star (ISEER 3.80 to 4.29) of split air conditioners in table 3.2(h) shall not be permitted for two-stage, multi-stage or variable capacity air conditioners from 1 January 2028 to 31 December 2029, to upgrade the minimum energy performance level of those units. Table (e) and (f) values are those printed in the amendment of 29 October 2019; the later amendments of 2020, 2021 and 2024 changed their periods, as described above.

Reading the table. The bands rise with each period: for example a split air conditioner needs an ISEER of at least 5.0 for 5 star in 2022 to 2025, at least 5.6 in 2026 to 2027 and at least 5.80 from 2028. A unit's label shows its ISEER and star level for the label series it was registered under, so check the label and the model's registration rather than comparing star counts across years. Retail and blog pages that quote other 2026 figures do not match the Gazette text above.

Is a 5 star AC from 2022 the same as a 5 star AC now?

Not necessarily: the printed bands changed between periods, as the table shows.

Does the star level tell me how much electricity I will use?

The ISEER behind it is a seasonal figure under test conditions; real use depends on many things.
