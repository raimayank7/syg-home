How to size an air conditioner

Sizing needs a load estimate. There is no official figure for it, so here is the method and what to ask.

What is official and what is not

Official: one ton of refrigeration is 3,517 watts; the unit's rated capacity is on the BEE label; the ISEER on the label is a seasonal measure tested at stated conditions. Not official: how many watts of cooling a room needs. No source that we could read gives an official cooling load per square metre, because the load depends on the building and the climate. The calculator on the home page therefore takes your own assumption.

Steps

  1. Measure the floor area of the room (length x width).
  2. List the things that add heat: people, computers, a kitchen, large sunny windows, a top-floor roof, west-facing walls.
  3. Choose a cooling per square metre you are willing to assume, and note that a hotter, sunnier or top-floor room needs more. Keep the choice written down.
  4. Add watts for specific heat sources you know.
  5. Convert to tons with 3,517 W per ton.
  6. Compare with the rated capacity on the label of candidate units.
  7. Ask an installer to confirm with a proper load calculation for your room.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by the name (1.5 ton) without reading the rated capacity.
  • Ignoring the heat from the sun on the roof or a west wall.
  • Treating one number as a rule for all rooms.

After sizing

Compare units by ISEER with the running cost calculator, using the number on each label.

Can the calculator replace an installer?

No. It is a starting point built on your assumption.

Should I round up to the next size?

This site gives no rule; ask the installer.
