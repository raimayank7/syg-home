What a ton of AC means

The "ton" of an AC is a unit of cooling capacity, not weight.

A unit of power

A ton of refrigeration is a unit of cooling capacity: the rate at which heat is removed. The BEE labelling specification for room air conditioners states that one ton of refrigeration is equivalent to 3,517 watts, and says the calculated tonnage on the label is rounded to two decimal places. Because a watt is a joule per second, a ton is not a weight at all.

Related units

1 Btu per hour is 0.29307107 watts (the definition of the Btu), so one ton is about 12,000 Btu per hour. A rated capacity of 5,275.5 watts is 1.5 tons; 7,034 watts is 2 tons.

Model names and labels

Air conditioners are named by tons (for example "1.5 ton"), but BEE requires the rated capacity to be shown on the label, and the label is the reference. Two units both called 1.5 ton can have slightly different rated capacities. Use the unit converter to compare.

The 2019 rule and the tonnage

The 24 degree default setting rule covers units up to 10,465 watts, about 2.98 tons: see the 24 degree default.

Why 3,517 watts?

It is the BEE figure for one ton of refrigeration in the label specification. The ton is historically defined by the cooling effect of melting a ton of ice in a day; BEE gives the watt equivalent to use.
