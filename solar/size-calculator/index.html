Rooftop Solar India

How many kW of rooftop solar do I need?

Type your monthly electricity units. The page shows the size the National Portal suggests and the size from the scheme's generation figure.

Two official pointers

The portal's sizing table. The home page of the National Portal lists the suitable rooftop solar capacity for the average monthly electricity consumption of a household: 0 to 150 units: 1 to 2 kW; 150 to 300 units: 2 to 3 kW; above 300 units: above 3 kW.

The scheme's generation figure. The Cabinet press release of 29 February 2024 says a 3 kW system "will be able to generate more than 300 units a month on average", about 100 units per kW per month. The calculator also divides your units by 100: a home using 250 units a month needs about 2.5 kW, one using 400 units about 4 kW.

Why both are only a starting point

Output depends on location, roof direction and tilt, shade, dust and equipment. The guidelines say the vendor and consumer decide the system design together, and the portal provides decision tools and vendor comparison. Ask the vendor for a written generation estimate for your own roof. Roof space and strength matter too: the DISCOM checks the installation against minimum technical specifications at inspection. This site has not read an official roof area per kilowatt, so it does not give one.

Sizing against the subsidy

The central subsidy stops at 3 kW. A larger system is allowed but gets no extra subsidy for the part above 3 kW. See the subsidy calculator and how much solar do I need.

These rules were read from the official portal and the scheme guidelines on 13 November 2026. The scheme runs until 31 March 2027, and rules and amounts can change: confirm on pmsuryaghar.gov.in before you pay anyone.

Should I size the system to cover all my units?

That depends on your tariff, roof and metering arrangement. Ask your vendor and distribution company.

Do applications for systems above 10 kW need feasibility approval?

The guidelines say that, under the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, 2020, applications for rooftop systems up to 10 kW are deemed accepted without technical feasibility approval, subject to the state regulator and DISCOM applying that provision; check locally.
