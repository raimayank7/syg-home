Solar payback explained

Payback is a single number that depends on three inputs you control: price, tariff and size.

The formula

Payback in years = what you pay / savings per year. What you pay is the quoted price minus the central subsidy (see how the subsidy works). Savings are the units generated in a year times the price per unit you avoid. At the scheme's figure of about 100 units per kW per month, a 3 kW system generates about 3,600 units a year.

The three levers

  • Price. A lower quote shortens payback, but check what is included: panels, inverter, mounting, wiring, metering, installation and portal paperwork.
  • Tariff. The higher the rate per unit you would otherwise pay, the larger the saving. Use the rate of the slab where your extra consumption falls.
  • Size. The subsidy is fixed per kW up to 3 kW, so it is larger per kW for small systems.

What simple payback hides

  • Interest if you borrow. See solar loans and costs for the portal's standard loan terms.
  • Tariff changes. A higher future rate shortens payback; a lower one lengthens it.
  • Panel ageing and maintenance.
  • Surplus power. What you are paid for units sent to the grid depends on the metering arrangement approved by your state regulator, which the guidelines say may be net metering, gross metering, net billing and others.

Comparing quotes fairly

Enter each quote in the payback calculator with the same tariff and size and compare the years. Prefer the quote that is clear and complete.

These rules were read from the official portal and the scheme guidelines on 13 November 2026. The scheme runs until 31 March 2027, and rules and amounts can change: confirm on pmsuryaghar.gov.in before you pay anyone.

What is a good payback?

This site gives no threshold; it depends on your circumstances.

Why do two homes with the same system get different paybacks?

Different tariffs and different units used.
