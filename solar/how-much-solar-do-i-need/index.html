How much rooftop solar does a home need?

Start from your bill, check against the roof, and compare with where the subsidy stops.

Step 1: your units

Look at the electricity bills of the last twelve months and note the units used each month. Summer and winter can differ a lot.

Step 2: convert to kilowatts

The National Portal's table: average monthly consumption 0 to 150 units: 1 to 2 kW; 150 to 300 units: 2 to 3 kW; above 300 units: above 3 kW. The Cabinet press release says a 3 kW system generates more than 300 units a month on average, so about 100 units per kW per month. The size calculator shows both.

Step 3: check the roof

Panels need unshaded roof space facing the sun and a roof strong enough to carry them. The guidelines place the system design with the consumer and vendor, and the DISCOM checks the installation against the minimum technical specifications. This site did not find an official area per kilowatt, so it does not give one; ask the vendor to measure your roof and show where the panels go.

Step 4: compare with the subsidy

The central subsidy stops at 3 kW. The guidelines also note that the CFA is calculated on the capacity of the solar modules, even if the installation includes extras such as batteries. Compare sizes in the payback calculator.

Step 5: ask the vendor

  • The expected units per month for your roof, in writing.
  • The make and warranty of panels and inverter, and the DCR (domestic content) certificate.
  • Who handles the net meter agreement and portal paperwork.
  • What the system does when the grid fails (ask; designs differ).

This is general information, not engineering advice.

Sources, all read on 13 November 2026: the National Portal pmsuryaghar.gov.in (home page, How to Apply, Financing Options, FAQs); the scheme guidelines "Central Financial Assistance to Residential Consumers" downloaded from the portal's Scheme Guidelines page; the PIB press release of 29 February 2024; the MNRE criteria annexure (PIB, August 2024). These rules were read from the official portal and the scheme guidelines on 13 November 2026. The scheme runs until 31 March 2027, and rules and amounts can change: confirm on pmsuryaghar.gov.in before you pay anyone.

Can I add a battery?

The guidelines say systems may include extra components such as battery storage, but the subsidy is based on the solar module capacity only.

Is a bigger system always better?

Not necessarily: it costs more, the roof may not fit it, and the subsidy stops at 3 kW.
