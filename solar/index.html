Rooftop solar subsidy calculator (PM-Surya Ghar)

Enter the size of your system in kW. The page applies the central subsidy rates from the official scheme guidelines.

What the calculator does

It applies the Central Financial Assistance (CFA) table of the scheme guidelines for a residential rooftop solar system: Rs 30,000 per kWp for the first 2 kWp (or part of it), Rs 18,000 per kWp for an additional 1 kWp or part of it, and no additional assistance beyond 3 kWp. For the "special category" states and Union Territories the rates are Rs 33,000 and Rs 19,800 per kWp: tick the box for them. The National Portal's home page shows the same figures (Rs 30,000 per kW up to 2 kW, Rs 18,000 per kW for additional capacity up to 3 kW, a maximum of Rs 78,000, and a 10% higher subsidy for special states).

The guidelines give worked examples that the calculator reproduces: 1.5 kW gets Rs 45,000; 2.5 kW gets Rs 30,000 x 2 + Rs 18,000 x 0.5 = Rs 69,000; 6 kW gets Rs 78,000. A part of a kilowatt counts in proportion.

Special category states

The guidelines name them: Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, the states of the North East including Sikkim, and the Union Territories of Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep.

What it does not include

  • System price. The guidelines state a benchmark cost of Rs 50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 45,000 for the additional kW (Rs 55,000 and Rs 49,500 in special category states), from which the subsidy is derived. The actual price you pay is agreed with your vendor, and this site does not print market prices.
  • State subsidy. The guidelines allow state governments to add their own subsidy, integrated with the National Portal. Amounts differ by state and are not on the national pages, so they are not included.
  • Housing societies. For group housing societies and resident welfare associations the guidelines give Rs 18,000 per kWp (Rs 19,800 in special states) for common facilities, at 3 kWp per house, up to 500 kWp. For example, an RWA of 20 houses installing 100 kW gets Rs 18,000 x 60 kW = Rs 10,80,000, the guidelines say.

These rules were read from the official portal and the scheme guidelines on 13 November 2026. The scheme runs until 31 March 2027, and rules and amounts can change: confirm on pmsuryaghar.gov.in before you pay anyone.

Is the subsidy a percentage of the cost?

The guidelines define it as 60% of the benchmark cost of the first 2 kWp and 40% of the benchmark cost of the additional kWp, which works out to the fixed rupee amounts per kWp above. It is calculated on capacity, not on the price you pay.

Does a 4 kW system get more subsidy than 3 kW?

No. There is no additional assistance beyond 3 kWp.

Can I get the subsidy and still use any panels?

The guidelines say the modules must meet the Domestic Content Requirement (domestically made modules from domestic cells); non-DCR modules make the installation ineligible for the subsidy. Consumers who give up the subsidy ("Give It Up") are free of that condition.
