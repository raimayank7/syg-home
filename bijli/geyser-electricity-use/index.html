How much electricity does a geyser use?

A geyser uses power in thousands of watts, but only while it is heating. What you pay depends on how much water you heat, and how long it sits hot.

The physics, simply

Heating water takes a fixed amount of energy: about 1.16 watt-hours to warm one litre by one degree Celsius. So:

Units to heat a tank = litres × temperature rise (°C) × 1.16 ÷ 1,000

Heating 25 litres from 20°C to 55°C, a rise of 35 degrees, takes 25 × 35 × 1.16 ÷ 1,000 ≈ 1 unit. A 15-litre geyser in the same conditions takes about 0.6 units; a 50-litre tank about 2 units. Winter tap water is colder, so the same tank takes more.

The geyser's wattage decides how fast it heats, not how much energy the job takes. A 2,000 W element heats that 25-litre tank in about half an hour; a 3,000 W element in about twenty minutes. Both use about the same units.

Why leaving it on costs money

A storage geyser keeps its water hot with a thermostat. Hot water slowly loses heat through the tank walls, the thermostat switches the element on again, and this repeats all day, bath or no bath. The loss depends on the tank's insulation: well-insulated, highly rated geysers lose little, and old or cheap ones lose a good deal. Either way, water heated at 6 am for a bath at 6 pm has been reheated for twelve hours to no purpose.

So: switch on 15 to 30 minutes before use, and off afterwards. A timer switch does this without anyone having to remember.

An instant geyser's high wattage looks alarming but it runs for minutes. Because it stores little, it wastes little. Its heavy draw needs proper wiring and a 16 A socket, and it counts towards your load.

Units for a household

Estimate the hot water you use, not the hours the switch is on. A bucket bath uses perhaps 15 to 20 litres of mixed water, of which about half is hot; a shower uses more, depending on its length. Four people, each using around 15 litres of hot water heated by 35 degrees, need about 60 litres: roughly 2.5 units a day, or 75 units a month in winter, plus standing losses. Put your own figures in the units calculator using the heating time, or measure with the meter.

Ways to use less

  1. Switch off after use, or fit a timer.
  2. Set the thermostat lower. Water at 50 to 55°C, used with little cold mixed in, loses less heat in the tank and pipes than water at 70°C. Very low settings for long periods are not advised for storage tanks; follow the maker's guidance.
  3. Right size. A 25-litre tank for one person's bucket bath heats water nobody uses. A small geyser, or an instant one, suits small needs.
  4. Buy a well-insulated, highly star-rated model. See star ratings.
  5. Short pipes, and insulated pipes where the run is long, so that less hot water is left to cool in them.
  6. Bathe one after another, so the tank is heated once.
  7. Fix dripping hot taps and a leaking pressure valve.
  8. Descale the tank and element where water is hard. Scale slows heating and shortens the element's life.

Alternatives

  • Solar water heater: a rooftop collector and insulated tank give hot water for most of the year in sunny places, with an electric back-up for cloudy days.
  • Heat pump water heater: uses a fraction of a geyser's electricity by moving heat from the air into the water, at a higher purchase price. Suited to large families.
  • Gas water heaters need good ventilation; never use one in a closed bathroom.
  • Immersion rods are cheap and efficient for a bucket, but demand care: plastic or metal bucket as the maker advises, switch off and unplug before touching the water, never leave unattended, keep away from children.

Safety

A geyser needs a proper earth connection and a working pressure release valve. A tingling shock from taps or shower water means a dangerous fault: switch off at the mains and call an electrician. An earth leakage breaker (RCCB) on the bathroom circuit saves lives.

How many units does a geyser use in one hour?

A 2,000 W geyser uses 2 units if its element is on for the full hour. In practice it switches off once the water is hot, so a 15 to 25 litre tank uses about 0.6 to 1 unit per heating.

Should I keep the geyser on all day?

No. Heat the water shortly before you need it and switch off.
