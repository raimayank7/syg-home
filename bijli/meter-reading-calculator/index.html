Meter reading calculator: units between two readings

Type the previous and present readings from your meter or bill. The page gives the units used and, with the two dates, your daily average.

The sum

Units used = present reading − previous reading

The meter counts upward for ever, like a car's odometer. The bill for a period is the difference between the reading at its end and the reading at its start. If the last bill ended at 4,512 and the meter now shows 4,768, you have used 256 units since then.

Add the two dates and the page also gives the units per day, and what that pace comes to in 30 and 60 days. That is the quickest way to know, in the middle of a billing period, what the next bill will look like, while there is still time to change it.

How to read your meter

Electronic (digital) meters, the common kind, have a small screen that cycles through several values every few seconds. Wait for the one marked kWh, usually with a long number. That is the cumulative reading.

  • Read only the whole number. Ignore digits after a decimal point.
  • Other screens show the date, time, voltage, current load in kW, maximum demand (MD) and sometimes kVAh. They are not the reading for a home bill, except that some suppliers bill larger connections in kVAh.
  • If the display is blank, a push button on the meter usually wakes it.

Older mechanical meters have a row of number wheels. Read the black digits and ignore the red one, which shows tenths.

Smart and prepaid meters show the same kWh figure, and your supplier's app usually shows daily use. See smart and prepaid meters.

Take a photograph of the meter with the date each time. It is useful evidence if a reading is ever disputed.

Check the bill against the meter

Your bill prints the previous reading, the present reading, the dates and the units billed. Once a bill arrives:

  1. Compare the present reading on the bill with what your meter showed on that date, or shows now. The meter should now read a little more than the bill's figure, never less.
  2. Check that the previous reading matches the present reading of the last bill.
  3. Check the status of the reading. Bills mark whether the meter was actually read or the units were estimated, with words or codes such as OK, Normal, Average, Provisional, Door locked or Defective. An estimated bill is adjusted when the next true reading is taken.
  4. Check the meter number on the bill against the number on your meter.

A present reading on the bill that is higher than the meter itself shows is a reading or typing error. Report it at once to the supplier with a dated photograph; this is one of the commonest causes of a wrongly high bill.

Keep your own record

Reading the meter on the same day each week takes a minute and tells you more than any bill:

  • a sudden jump points to a new habit, a faulty appliance or a leak to earth;
  • you learn what a week of air conditioning or a month of geyser use really costs;
  • you can see whether changes you make are working.

To find what one appliance uses, note the reading, run only that appliance for an hour with everything else off, and note it again. On many digital meters the kWh display shows one or two decimal places for exactly this purpose.

A quick meter check

Switch off the main switch, or every appliance and light. The meter should stop counting, and its pulse light should stop blinking. If it keeps advancing with everything off, something is still drawing current, possibly through wiring you do not know about, or there is a fault. Ask an electrician to check the wiring, and the supplier to test the meter, which they do on request under their rules.

How do I calculate units from a meter reading?

Subtract the previous reading from the present reading. The difference is the units, in kWh.

Which number on the meter is the reading?

The value marked kWh on a digital meter, without the digits after the decimal point.

What if my bill shows an average or estimated reading?

The supplier could not read the meter and has estimated the units. It is corrected when an actual reading is taken; you can also give your own reading through the supplier's app or office where that is allowed.
