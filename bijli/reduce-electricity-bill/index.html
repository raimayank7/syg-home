How to reduce your electricity bill

Find the two or three appliances that make up most of your bill and change how they are used. Everything else is small.

Start by measuring

Use the units calculator to see where your units go. In most homes the order is: air conditioning, water heating, the refrigerator, fans, then everything else. Work down that list. And remember that under slab rates, the units you save are the costliest ones you buy. See how slab rates work.

Air conditioner

  1. Set it to 24 to 26°C, not 18. Each degree lower makes the compressor work longer. A ceiling fan on low with the air conditioner at 26°C feels as cool as a much lower setting.
  2. Keep the room closed: doors and windows shut, curtains drawn against the afternoon sun, gaps under doors sealed.
  3. Clean the filters every two weeks in the season. Clogged filters make the unit run longer for the same cooling.
  4. Use the timer or sleep mode at night, so that it eases off towards morning.
  5. Service it before summer, and shade the outdoor unit while leaving it room to breathe.
  6. When buying, choose an inverter model with a high star rating, sized for the room. See AC electricity use.

Water heating

  1. Switch the geyser on a little before the bath and off after it. A geyser left on all day keeps reheating water that nobody uses.
  2. Lower the thermostat to a comfortable temperature instead of mixing very hot water with cold.
  3. Fix dripping hot taps, and keep baths short in winter.
  4. Consider a solar water heater if you have a roof and sun. See geyser electricity use.

Refrigerator

  1. Set the dial to the middle, not the coldest, except in peak summer.
  2. Keep it away from the wall and from heat, with space behind for air.
  3. Check the door seal: a sheet of paper shut in the door should be held firmly.
  4. Do not put hot food in, and do not leave the door open while deciding.
  5. Defrost direct-cool models when ice builds up. An old fridge of fifteen years or more can use two or three times the electricity of a new efficient one.

Fans, lights and the rest

  1. Replace ordinary ceiling fans that run many hours with energy-saving BLDC fans, which use well under half the power. Start with the fans that run longest.
  2. Change remaining tube lights and bulbs to LED.
  3. Switch off at the wall: set-top boxes, televisions, chargers, music systems and microwave ovens draw a little all day on standby. One switch for the television corner makes it easy.
  4. Run the washing machine full, with cold water for ordinary loads. Iron in one go, not a few clothes at a time.
  5. Use the pressure cooker and lids, and match the pan to the induction ring. Fill the kettle only with what you need.

Things that are not appliances

  • Water pump: an automatic level controller stops overflow and wasted pumping.
  • Inverter batteries: an old, failing battery bank keeps the charger working all day. See inverter power use.
  • Wiring: old or loose wiring and an earth leakage waste power and are dangerous. Have an electrician check a house whose meter runs with everything off.
  • Sanctioned load: if it is far above what you need, you are paying extra fixed charge. See fixed charge.
  • Bill errors: check the reading on each bill against the meter. See why is my bill high.
  • Rebates: some suppliers give a small rebate for online or early payment, or for prepaid meters. Check your supplier's page.

Beware of "power saver" devices

Plug-in boxes sold as electricity savers for homes do not reduce a home bill, which is charged on real energy in kWh. No device plugged into a socket can make your appliances use less.

Solar on the roof

A rooftop solar system with net metering can cut the units you buy from the grid. Whether it pays depends on your roof, your consumption and current government schemes. See rooftop solar basics.

What uses the most electricity at home?

Air conditioners, geysers and heaters, then the refrigerator and fans because they run so many hours.

Does switching appliances off at the socket save much?

Standby power for a household's gadgets adds up to a few units a month. It is worth doing, but small beside air conditioning and water heating.
