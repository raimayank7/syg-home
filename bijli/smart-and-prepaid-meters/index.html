Smart meters and prepaid meters explained

A smart meter measures the same units as the old meter. What changes is that it reports them by itself, and can be run on advance payment.

What a smart meter is

A smart meter is an electronic meter with a communication link, usually a built-in mobile SIM or radio. It records consumption in short intervals and sends the readings to the supplier automatically. Nobody has to come to read it, and the supplier and you can both see consumption day by day.

It can work in two ways:

  • Postpaid: you are billed after use, as before. Only the reading is automatic.
  • Prepaid: you pay in advance, like a mobile recharge, and the balance falls as you use electricity.

The tariff itself, slabs and fixed charge, is set by the regulator and is the same kind of calculation in both modes; some regulators allow a small rebate for prepaid consumers. Your supplier's tariff schedule says what applies.

Reading the display

The screen scrolls through several values. The main ones:

DisplayMeaning
kWhTotal units recorded so far. This is the reading
kW or WThe load being drawn at this moment
MDMaximum demand recorded in the period
Date and timeThe meter's clock
Balance (prepaid)Rupees or units left

A blinking light on the meter flashes faster as you draw more power.

Using the supplier's app

With a smart meter, the supplier's app or website normally shows:

  • daily and sometimes hourly consumption, as a chart;
  • the current balance (prepaid) or the bill so far;
  • recharge and payment history;
  • alerts for low balance and unusual use.

This is the smart meter's real benefit to a household. You can see what yesterday's air conditioning or geyser use came to, and catch a fault within days instead of at the next bill. Install only the official app named on your bill or the supplier's website.

How prepaid works

  1. You recharge through the supplier's app, website, bill-payment apps or a counter.
  2. The meter or the supplier's system deducts the cost of each day's units, together with a daily share of the fixed charge and other charges, from the balance.
  3. You receive alerts as the balance runs low.
  4. If the balance reaches zero, supply is disconnected under the supplier's rules, and restored after a recharge. Regulations in many states provide protections, such as no disconnection at night or on holidays, and a small emergency credit; the rules differ, so read your supplier's.

Keep a comfortable balance before travelling, so that the fridge does not go off while you are away.

Why a bill can look higher after a smart meter

  • Old meters ran slow. Worn mechanical and early electronic meters sometimes under-recorded. An accurate meter then shows the true, higher figure.
  • No more estimated bills. Consumption that used to be averaged is now billed in full, in the month it happens.
  • Arrears from the old meter are recovered, sometimes in instalments from prepaid recharges.
  • Fixed charges deducted daily in prepaid mode make the balance fall even on days of no use.
  • A changed billing cycle in the month of change-over makes one bill cover an unusual number of days.

If you still doubt the meter, ask for a test against a check meter, as with any meter. See why is my bill high.

Common worries

  • "Does the meter itself use my electricity?" A meter's own small consumption is on the supplier's side of the measurement; you are billed for what passes through to your house.
  • "Can it be switched off remotely?" Yes. Smart meters have a switch that the supplier can operate, for disconnection and reconnection under the rules.
  • "Is my data private?" The meter sends consumption readings to the supplier, which is bound by the regulator's rules on consumer data.
  • "Will a recharge agent or a caller need my OTP?" No. Recharge yourself through official channels. Anyone who calls to "update your meter" or "avoid disconnection" and asks you to install an app or share a code is a fraudster. See the electricity bill scam.

Net meters

A home with rooftop solar has a net meter (or a smart meter set up for net metering) that counts units taken from the grid and units sent to it separately, usually shown as "import" and "export". The bill is for the difference, under the supplier's net-metering rules. See rooftop solar basics.

Is a prepaid meter costlier than a postpaid one?

The tariff is set by the regulator and is worked out in the same way. Some states give prepaid consumers a small rebate. What changes is that you pay before use.

What happens when a prepaid balance becomes zero?

Supply is disconnected under the supplier's rules and restored after recharge. Many states bar disconnection at night or on holidays and allow a small emergency credit; check your supplier's rules.
