What is one unit of electricity (kWh)?

One unit is one kilowatt-hour: the electricity used by a 1,000-watt appliance in one hour.

Watts, kilowatts and units

  • A watt (W) measures how fast an appliance uses electricity at a given moment. A bulb marked 9 W uses little; a geyser marked 2,000 W uses a lot.
  • A kilowatt (kW) is 1,000 watts. The geyser is a 2 kW appliance.
  • A unit, or kilowatt-hour (kWh), measures how much electricity has been used over time. It is what the meter counts and what you pay for.

Think of water: watts are how wide the tap is open, and units are how much water has run into the bucket.

The formula

Units = watts × hours ÷ 1,000

How long does one unit last?

Divide 1,000 by the watts to get the hours one unit will run an appliance. A 50-watt fan: 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20 hours. A 1,200-watt microwave oven: 1,000 ÷ 1,200 = 0.83 hours, or 50 minutes.

Why heat is expensive

Anything that makes heat or moves heat uses power in thousands of watts: geysers, room heaters, irons, kettles, induction cooktops, air conditioners. Lights, fans, televisions and chargers use tens of watts. This is why a household's bill is decided mostly by a few appliances, and why winter geyser use and summer air conditioning show up so sharply.

Units on the meter and the bill

The meter's main display, marked kWh, is the running total of units since it was installed. A bill charges for the difference between two readings. See the meter reading calculator.

Your sanctioned load, by contrast, is in kW: it is about how much you may draw at one moment, not how much you use over the month. A home can have a 5 kW load and use 150 units or 600. See fixed charge and sanctioned load.

Other terms you may see

  • kVA and kVAh: used for large connections. They measure "apparent" power, which is a little more than the real power in kW when equipment such as motors is inefficient in the way it draws current. Some suppliers bill bigger connections in kVAh.
  • MD (maximum demand): the highest load drawn in the period, in kW or kVA.
  • Ampere (A): the current. At about 230 volts, 1 ampere is roughly 230 watts. A 16 A socket is meant for heavy appliances and a 6 A socket for light ones.
  • HP (horsepower): used for motors and pumps. One HP is 746 watts of output; a motor draws somewhat more than that from the supply.

Working out your own use

List your appliances, their watts and the hours each runs, and add up the units. The units calculator does this, with typical wattages filled in. The wattage chart lists common appliances.

How many watts make one unit?

A unit is not a number of watts but watts over time: 1,000 watts for one hour, 500 watts for two hours, or 100 watts for ten hours.

How many units does a house use in a month?

It varies widely with the number of people, the climate and above all air conditioning and water heating. Your own bill's consumption history is the only reliable guide for your home.

Is kWh the same as a unit?

Yes. "Unit" is the everyday word for one kilowatt-hour.
