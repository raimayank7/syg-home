Fixed charge and sanctioned load explained

The fixed charge pays for keeping the supply ready for you. It depends on your sanctioned load, not on the units you use.

Two parts of every bill

An electricity bill has an energy charge, for the units you used, and a fixed charge (also called demand charge), a monthly amount that is the same whether you used a great deal or nothing. The fixed charge meets the cost of the wires, transformers and staff that keep power available at your door at all hours.

That is why a locked house still gets a bill.

How the fixed charge is worked out

Most suppliers charge a set amount per kW of sanctioned load, per month, and the amount per kW is often higher for bigger loads. Some charge a flat amount per connection instead, and some charge on the maximum demand actually recorded.

An official example: in Punjab for FY 2026-27, the fixed charge for domestic supply is ₹50 per kW per month for loads up to 2 kW, ₹70 per kW above 2 kW and up to 7 kW, and ₹100 per kW above 7 kW and up to 20 kW, and the official schedule says the fixed charge is levied on 80% of the sanctioned load. So a 5 kW home connection there pays 5 × 0.8 × ₹70 = ₹280 a month as fixed charge. (Source and date: the Punjab tariff page.) Your own supplier's rule is printed in its tariff schedule and shown on the bill.

What sanctioned load is

When a connection is given, the supplier approves a sanctioned load in kilowatts: the most your premises are expected to draw at one time. It decides the size of the meter and service cable, whether you get single-phase or three-phase supply, the fixed charge, and the security deposit.

It is not the same as:

  • connected load, the total wattage of everything you own, which is always more than you use at once;
  • maximum demand (MD), the highest load you actually drew in the period, recorded by the meter and printed on the bill.

Estimating the load you need

  1. List the appliances that may run at the same time on a heavy day: for example an air conditioner, the fridge, a geyser or iron, fans, lights and the television.
  2. Add their watts and divide by 1,000 for kilowatts. The wattage chart helps.
  3. Compare with the MD on your recent bills, which is the real measure.

A small flat with no air conditioner manages on 1 to 2 kW. A home with one or two air conditioners and a geyser commonly needs several kW. Supply rules also set the load above which three-phase supply is given; your supplier's office will tell you its limit.

When the load is too low

If your recorded maximum demand keeps exceeding the sanctioned load, suppliers may bill a penalty, revise the load themselves, or both, under their supply code. Signs you need more: the main breaker trips when two heavy appliances run together, lights dim when the air conditioner starts, or the bill shows MD above the sanctioned figure. Apply for load enhancement; it involves a form, charges under the supplier's schedule and sometimes an additional deposit, and may need the wiring certified.

When the load is too high

If the house has a far larger sanctioned load than it uses, perhaps because a shop or a tenant's equipment has gone, you are paying fixed charges for capacity you do not need. Apply for load reduction. Check first that the lower load still covers your heavy days, and how your supplier's fixed-charge bands fall: dropping to a lower band can lower the rate per kW as well.

The security deposit

Suppliers hold a deposit against each connection, related to the load or to typical consumption, and review it from time to time; a notice or bill line asking for "additional security deposit" follows a rise in consumption. Many suppliers credit interest on the deposit to the bill once a year. Their supply code gives the rule.

Other fixed items

Meter rent, where charged, and minimum charges, where a tariff has them, are also independent of consumption. A minimum charge means that if your energy charge falls below a set figure, the set figure is billed instead.

Why is there a bill when the house was locked all month?

Because of the fixed charge, which is payable every month while the connection exists, plus meter rent where charged.

How do I reduce the fixed charge?

Only by reducing the sanctioned load, if it is more than you need, or by surrendering a connection you do not use.

Where is my sanctioned load written?

Near the top of the bill, as "sanctioned load", "connected load" or "contract demand", in kW.
