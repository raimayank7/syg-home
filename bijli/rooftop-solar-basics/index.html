Rooftop solar for homes: the basics

Solar panels on the roof make electricity by day. With net metering, what you do not use at once is credited against what you draw at night.

How a home system works

  1. Panels on the roof turn sunlight into direct current.
  2. An inverter converts it into ordinary household current.
  3. The house uses that electricity first. Any surplus flows out to the grid.
  4. A net meter records what you import from the grid and what you export to it.
  5. The bill is for the net of the two, under the supplier's net-metering rules, plus fixed charges, which continue.

This is a grid-connected system, the common and cheaper kind. It has no battery and, for safety, switches off during a power cut. A hybrid system adds batteries to keep some circuits running in a cut, at a higher price. An off-grid system, with batteries and no grid link, suits places with no reliable supply.

What size do I need?

Systems are sized in kilowatts (kW) of panel capacity. Output depends on the sun at your place and the season. A common planning figure for much of India is that 1 kW of panels yields roughly 4 units a day averaged over the year, more in clear summer weather and less in the monsoon and in winter fog. An installer will give a figure for your location.

To size from your bill: take your average units per month from the consumption history on the bill, and divide by about 120 (30 days × 4 units). A home using 360 units a month would look at about 3 kW. Regulations usually cap the system at your sanctioned load or a share of it, so check your load.

What the roof must offer

  • Shadow-free space, as a rough guide about 8 to 10 square metres for each kW, facing south where possible. Shade from a tank, a neighbour's wall or a tree cuts output sharply.
  • A sound roof that can take the structure and its wind load.
  • Access for cleaning.
  • The right to use the roof: in flats, the society's consent.

What it does to the bill

  • The units you generate replace units you would have bought. Under slab tariffs these are your costliest units, since solar trims consumption from the top. See how slab rates work.
  • Fixed charges continue, and so do any minimum charges.
  • Surplus exported beyond your own use is credited or paid for at a rate set by the regulator, often lower than the rate you pay; how credits carry forward and are settled differs from state to state.

Whether and when the system pays for itself depends on its price, any government subsidy, your tariff and how much of the output you use yourself. Government support schemes for home rooftop solar exist and change; take the current terms only from the official national portal for rooftop solar and your own distribution company, not from an installer's leaflet.

The steps to install

  1. Check your bill for consumption and sanctioned load.
  2. Apply for net metering through the official portal or your distribution company. Approval of technical feasibility comes first.
  3. Choose an installer from the list of vendors registered or empanelled with the distribution company or the official portal.
  4. Installation, then inspection by the distribution company and fitting of the net meter.
  5. Commissioning, after which generation starts counting. Any subsidy is claimed through the official process.

Do not let an installer switch on a grid-connected system before the net meter is fitted: exported units may be counted as consumption by an ordinary meter.

Questions to ask an installer

  • What yearly output in units do you estimate for my roof, in writing?
  • Which panels and inverter, and what are their warranties? Panels usually carry a long performance warranty; inverters a shorter one.
  • What is included: structure, wiring, earthing, lightning protection, net-meter charges, approvals?
  • Who handles the net-metering application and subsidy paperwork?
  • What service and cleaning support is offered, and at what cost after the first year?
  • Can I speak to earlier customers nearby?

Looking after it

Wash the panels with plain water every few weeks, early in the morning or in the evening; dust can cut output noticeably in dry months. Keep growing trees from shading them. Watch the inverter's display or app: a sudden drop in daily units on a clear day means a fault. Compare the export figure on each bill with the inverter's own count.

Beware

Rooftop solar attracts fraud: fake "government scheme" websites and callers who take an advance for registration or promise free panels. Apply only through the official portal and your distribution company, and pay only a registered vendor against a proper invoice.

Does solar work during a power cut?

An ordinary grid-connected system switches off in a cut, for the safety of linemen. Only hybrid or off-grid systems with batteries keep supplying.

Will my bill become zero with solar?

The energy charge can fall to very little if generation matches consumption, but fixed charges and some levies continue.

How many units does a 1 kW solar system make?

Roughly 4 units a day on average over a year in much of India, varying with place, season, shade and cleanliness.
