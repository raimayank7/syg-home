How to read your electricity bill

Bills differ in layout from one supplier to another, but they all carry the same dozen facts. Once you know them, any bill can be read in a minute.

Who you are

On the billWhat it is
Consumer number / account number / CA number / K numberThe number that identifies your connection. You need it to pay, complain or check anything online. The name differs by supplier
Name and addressOf the registered consumer. If you bought or inherited the property, get the name changed; it matters for subsidies and proof of address
Tariff categoryThe kind of connection: domestic, non-domestic or commercial, industrial, agricultural. The rates follow from this, so check it is right
Sanctioned load / connected loadThe load, in kW, that your connection is approved for. The fixed charge is usually based on it. See fixed charge and sanctioned load
Meter numberShould match the number on your meter
PhaseSingle phase for most homes; three phase for larger loads

What you used

On the billWhat it is
Previous reading and dateThe meter's figure at the end of the last bill
Present reading and dateThe meter's figure now
Units consumedPresent minus previous, in kWh. Check it yourself with the meter reading calculator
Multiplying factor (MF)Normally 1 for homes. For large connections the difference in readings is multiplied by this number
Reading statusWhether the meter was actually read (OK, Normal) or the units estimated (Average, Provisional, Locked, Defective)
Bill period / billing daysThe dates covered. Many suppliers bill homes every month, some every two months
Maximum demand (MD)The highest load you drew in the period, in kW. If it stays above your sanctioned load, the supplier may revise the load
Consumption historyUnits in earlier months, often as a small chart. Useful for spotting a sudden rise

What you are charged

On the billWhat it is
Energy chargeUnits multiplied by the rate, slab by slab. See how slab rates work
Fixed charge / demand chargeA monthly amount that does not depend on units, usually per kW of load
Fuel adjustment (FPPCA, FPPAS, FAC, PPAC and similar names)A per-unit amount, added or occasionally reduced, that passes on changes in the cost of fuel and purchased power. It is revised from time to time
Electricity duty / taxLevied by the state government on consumption
Meter rent / service chargeA small monthly amount with some suppliers
Cess, surcharge, municipal taxOther levies, where a state has them
RebatesFor paying on time or online, for prepaid meters, or other schemes, where offered
SubsidyAn amount borne by the state government and deducted from your bill, where a scheme applies

What you must pay

On the billWhat it is
Current bill amountThis period's charges
Arrears / previous balanceUnpaid amounts from earlier bills. If you have paid, check the date: a payment made after the bill was prepared shows in the next one
Late payment surchargeAdded when an earlier bill was paid late
AdjustmentsCorrections of earlier estimated or wrong bills, credits and debits
Security deposit and interest on itThe deposit held against your connection. Interest on it is credited once a year by many suppliers, and the deposit may be revised from your consumption
Net amount payableWhat to pay by the due date
Due date, and amount after due datePaying after the date costs more, and long-unpaid bills lead to disconnection after notice

Check these five things every time

  1. The present reading is not more than your meter shows.
  2. The reading status is actual, not estimated month after month.
  3. The units are in line with the same months of last year.
  4. Arrears are nil if you paid the last bill.
  5. The tariff category and load are yours.

If something is wrong

Write to the supplier, through its app, website or sub-division office, with the consumer number, the bill, and a dated photograph of the meter. Keep the complaint number. Every supplier has a consumer grievance forum, and beyond it an electricity ombudsman, whose addresses are printed on the bill or the supplier's website. Pay the bill, or the undisputed part, by the due date under the supplier's rules while the complaint is examined, so that no surcharge builds up.

Paying safely

Pay only through the supplier's own website or app, your bank's or UPI app's bill payment section, or the supplier's counter. A text message that threatens disconnection tonight and gives a mobile number to call is a fraud; see our sister site on the electricity bill scam.

What is the consumer number on an electricity bill?

The unique number of your connection, printed near the top. It may be called account number, CA number, K number or service number.

What is FPPCA or fuel surcharge?

A per-unit adjustment that passes on changes in the cost of fuel and power bought by the supplier. Its name and amount differ by state and change over time.
