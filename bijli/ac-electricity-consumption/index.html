How much electricity does an air conditioner use?

An air conditioner's use depends less on its label than on the room, the weather and the temperature you set.

What the numbers on an AC mean

  • Tonnage (1 ton, 1.5 ton, 2 ton) is cooling capacity, not weight or power use. One ton of cooling is about 3,500 watts of heat removed. A bigger tonnage cools a bigger room.
  • Rated power input, in watts, is what the unit draws from the socket at full load. It is on the rating plate and in the brochure. As a broad guide, modern 1 ton units draw around 900 to 1,200 W at full load and 1.5 ton units around 1,300 to 1,800 W; older and less efficient units draw more.
  • The star label gives a tested efficiency figure and the units used in a year under standard conditions. This is the best single number for comparing models. See star ratings.

Why the same AC uses different amounts

An air conditioner does not draw full power all the time. Once the room reaches the set temperature, a fixed-speed unit switches its compressor off and on, and an inverter unit slows its compressor down. How much it runs depends on:

  1. The set temperature. The lower the setting, the longer the compressor works.
  2. The heat outside, and whether the room is on the top floor or faces the afternoon sun.
  3. The room: size, insulation, curtains, gaps, and how often the door opens.
  4. People and appliances in the room, each adding heat.
  5. The condition of the unit: clean filters and coils, correct gas charge.
  6. Sizing. An undersized unit runs flat out and never rests; a badly oversized one cools in bursts and controls humidity poorly.

Estimating units for your own AC

Rough method. Take the rated input in watts from the plate. In a typical evening and night of use, the average draw over the hours may be around half to three-quarters of that. Multiply by the hours and divide by 1,000.

Example: a unit rated 1,500 W, used 8 hours, averaging two-thirds of full load: 1,500 × 0.67 × 8 ÷ 1,000 ≈ 8 units a night. Over 30 nights, about 240 units. Your own figure may be well above or below this.

Better method. Read the meter before and after a night of normal use with other heavy appliances off, and subtract what the fridge and fans use. Or look at the supplier's app if you have a smart meter. See the meter reading calculator.

For comparing models, use the yearly units on the star label, not the tonnage.

Put the monthly units into the bill calculator to see the cost at your own slab rates; under slabs, air conditioner units are usually billed at your highest rate.

Inverter or fixed speed?

An inverter AC varies its compressor speed to hold the temperature steadily, and uses noticeably less electricity than a fixed-speed unit of the same capacity in long hours of use. It costs more to buy. The longer your daily use, the sooner the difference is recovered. For a room cooled an hour or two a day, the gain is small.

Settings and habits that cut use

  • 24 to 26°C with a fan on low is comfortable for most people and far cheaper than 20°C.
  • Sleep or eco mode raises the temperature slightly through the night.
  • Do not set a very low temperature to cool faster. The unit cools at the same rate; it only overshoots and wastes power.
  • Close the room, draw curtains, switch off unneeded lights and gadgets.
  • Clean filters fortnightly; service before the season.
  • Shade the outdoor unit without blocking its airflow.
  • Switch it off at the mains when away for days; the electronics draw a little on standby.
  • Use the timer to stop cooling shortly before you wake.

Coolers and fans compared

An air cooler draws roughly a tenth of an air conditioner's power and a ceiling fan less still. In dry heat a cooler works well; in humid weather it does not. Using the fan with a higher AC setting gets the comfort of both for less.

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use in 8 hours?

Very roughly 6 to 10 units for a reasonably efficient inverter model in ordinary summer conditions, more in extreme heat, on a top floor or at a low temperature setting. Measure your own with the meter.

Does an AC use more power at 18°C than at 24°C?

Yes. The compressor runs longer and harder to hold a lower temperature.

Is it cheaper to leave the AC on all day than to switch it on and off?

No. Switch it off when the room is empty for more than a short while.
