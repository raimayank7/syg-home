How calories burned are calculated from METs

One formula, three numbers: the MET value, your weight and the time.

The formula

The Compendium of Physical Activities website gives the conversions this site uses (page "Unit Conversions", read 13 November 2026):

  • 1 MET = 3.5 millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute.
  • 1 MET = 1 kilocalorie per kilogram per hour.
  • METs to kcal per minute = METs x 3.5 x body weight in kg / 200.

So the kilocalories burned in a session are METs x 3.5 x body weight (kg) / 200 x minutes. The factor 3.5 / 200 = 0.0175 kcal per kg per minute at 1 MET, which is 1.05 kcal per kg per hour; the Compendium states the simpler rule of 1 kcal per kg per hour, and the two differ by 5 per cent. This site uses the 3.5 / 200 formula that the Compendium gives for METs to kcal per minute.

A worked example

A person of 70 kg doing an activity of 8 METs for 30 minutes: 8 x 3.5 x 70 / 200 = 9.8 kcal per minute, and 9.8 x 30 = 294 kcal. At 60 kg the same session gives 252 kcal; at 80 kg, 336 kcal. Weight matters in proportion: the formula is a straight line, so 10 per cent more body weight means 10 per cent more kilocalories for the same activity and time.

MET-minutes

MET-minutes are the MET value multiplied by the minutes (a 3.5 MET activity for 30 minutes is 105 MET-minutes). They do not depend on body weight, which is why the physical activity guidelines use them. See how much activity guidelines suggest.

How long to use a given number of kilocalories

Turn the formula around: minutes = kilocalories / (METs x 3.5 x kg / 200). A 70 kg person at 8 METs uses 9.8 kcal per minute, so 100 kcal takes about 10.2 minutes. The calculator on the home page has a box for this.

Source: Ainsworth BE et al., 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities (Journal of Sport and Health Science, January 2024), pacompendium.com, the PDF "2024 Adult Compendium" read on 13 November 2026; unit conversions from the Compendium website (pacompendium.com/unite-conversions); intensity classes from the Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (US Department of Health and Human Services, 2018), read on 13 November 2026. The Compendium states that the Adult Compendium, Older Adult Compendium and Wheelchair Compendium are free to use for commercial purposes and asks users not to change MET values: none is changed here.

Why not "weight in kg times MET times hours"?

That simpler rule is the 1 kcal per kg per hour statement and gives a figure about 5 per cent lower; the page above gives the 3.5 / 200 formula for METs to kcal per minute, which this site uses.

Does the figure include the calories I would use anyway?

Yes. A MET value counts the whole energy cost, including the resting part, so the figure is total energy during the activity, not extra energy above rest.

An estimate. MET values are averages for adults, not measurements of you. Treat every figure as a rough estimate. This site gives general information, not medical advice, and is independent of the Compendium authors and of the US government. See accuracy and limits.
