What is a MET?

A MET compares an activity with sitting at rest.

The definition

The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd edition, 2018) say that energy expenditure is expressed by multiples of the metabolic equivalent of task (MET), where 1 MET is the rate of energy expenditure while sitting at rest, which generally corresponds to an oxygen uptake of 3.5 millilitres per kilogram of body weight per minute. The Compendium of Physical Activities uses the same 3.5 figure as its reference for 1 MET.

An activity of 6 METs therefore uses about six times as much energy per minute as sitting at rest.

The Compendium

The Compendium of Physical Activities was first developed in 1989 and published in 1993, 2000, 2011 and, as the 2024 update, in January 2024. This site uses the 2024 Adult Compendium, which lists 1110 activity rows (as read from the PDF) in 22 major headings, each with a five-digit code, a MET value and a description.

Intensity classes

The Physical Activity Guidelines define intensity by METs:

ClassMETsExamples from the guidelines
Sedentary behaviour1.5 or less, while sitting, reclining or lyingsitting
Lightless than 3.0walking at 2 mph or less, cooking, light household chores
Moderate3.0 to 5.9walking briskly (2.5 to 4 mph), mopping or vacuuming, raking the yard
Vigorous6.0 or morerunning, shovelling snow, a strenuous fitness class

The classes are used on every page of this site. Note that the guidelines apply these classes to activity done by healthy adults: a person with lower fitness may find a moderate activity harder than the class suggests.

Source: Ainsworth BE et al., 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities (Journal of Sport and Health Science, January 2024), pacompendium.com, the PDF "2024 Adult Compendium" read on 13 November 2026; unit conversions from the Compendium website (pacompendium.com/unite-conversions); intensity classes from the Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (US Department of Health and Human Services, 2018), read on 13 November 2026. The Compendium states that the Adult Compendium, Older Adult Compendium and Wheelchair Compendium are free to use for commercial purposes and asks users not to change MET values: none is changed here.

Is a MET the same for everyone?

The standard MET value is the same for everyone by definition, but the energy a person really uses differs with body size, fitness and technique. That is why every figure here is an estimate.

What is 1 MET in calories?

By the Compendium's conversion, 1 MET is about 1 kcal per kilogram per hour (more exactly 1.05 with the 3.5 / 200 factor).

An estimate. MET values are averages for adults, not measurements of you. Treat every figure as a rough estimate. This site gives general information, not medical advice, and is independent of the Compendium authors and of the US government. See accuracy and limits.
